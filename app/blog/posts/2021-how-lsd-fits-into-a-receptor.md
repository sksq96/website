In April of 2021 I wrote a small post entitled 'A photo of LSD inside a human brain receptor'. My post was a summary of a research paper where the researchers had taken a picture of a single LSD molecule inside a human serotonin receptor (5-HT2B) using a technique called X-ray crystallography. It's a stunning image. I've been to Burning Man and taken LSD there, and I know that the drug can cause visual and physical distortions, can cause you to experience highs and lows, to experience sensations of ego dissolution, and can leave you feeling unmoored from yourself and the world. And it's hard to look at an image like this, or to read the paper, and not ask yourself how this one picture of a molecule inside a protein can cause all of those effects. It's a question you can ask, but it's not a question you can answer yet.

But what you can do with this picture is put it into the context of a much larger body of work, which is trying to understand how small molecules, like drugs, bind to the proteins in our cells and cause the effects that drugs have.

The image above of LSD inside the 5-HT2B receptor (more on this in a second) is from this paper by Wacker et al. (2017).

In the paper, Wacker et al. purified the 5-HT2B receptor, and then made it so the receptor could bind to LSD and would not fall apart. Making proteins fit into a conformation where they can bind to other proteins and molecules is a big deal in structural biology.

Then, they crystallized the receptor, which is when proteins are taken and made into crystals. The crystal is then taken and blasted with X-rays. Because the atoms in the protein diffract the X-rays, this can be used to create an electron density map of the atoms in the protein. The paper reports that the electron density map was made at a resolution of 3.5Å.

If you want to look at the crystal structure yourself, it's been uploaded to the Protein Data Bank (PDB).

The 5-HT2B receptor is a membrane protein, meaning that it is embedded in the cell membrane. Like other G-protein coupled receptor proteins (GPCRs), it is a transmembrane protein that spans the lipid bilayer. GPCRs have a characteristic structure of seven α helices that span the lipid bilayer (and contain extracellular loops). The extracellular loops of the 5-HT2B receptor form the LSD binding pocket, and the LSD molecule is buried in the binding pocket.
