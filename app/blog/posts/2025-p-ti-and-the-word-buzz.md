In January, I was looking up _piti buzz_.

The only time I’ve ever heard the word “buzz” used is in the context of cannabis.

You can use the word to talk about how weed “buzzes” through your body. The word evokes the texture and spread of the buzz, as well as the “thickness” or “strength” of it. The word is a bit wishy-washy when it comes to the strength and spread. You can have a light buzz, and you can have an intense buzz. You can have a small buzz, and you can have a full-body buzz.

But given all these uncertainties, the “texture” of the buzz still remains. You can visualize it, or at least get an image of it: buzzing, buzzing, buzzing. But what does it mean to be buzzing? It’s hard to tell - but I can feel the abstraction, I do have a sense of it.

In the text I was reading, pīti was described as a kind of “buzzing,” or “thrilling,” feeling that spreads throughout the body.

But I did not quite understand what was meant by the word “buzzing,” because I was not sure if this word was inextricably related to weed.

It turns out that it isn’t.

I can feel the buzz of pīti in my body, even when I’m sober.

I think I would have been a lot more interested in cannabis had I been able to recognize this buzz without using the substance.

You can read more about pīti in _The Joy of Meditation_:

> Imagine that you’ve been in a hot, stuffy room for several hours and then you step outside into the cool, fresh air. The first thing that happens is that you feel a sense of excitement and thrill, a sense of relief and refreshment. This is piti. This type of piti is called “minor piti.” So we see that piti is the pleasant feeling that precedes real joy. Piti can also be likened to the exhilaration a farmer feels when he sees dark clouds forming just before the rains come to relieve a long drought. Or it’s like the excitement a thirsty man feels when he sees a mirage of water in the desert.

You can also read more about it in _The Mind Illuminated_:

> Piti can be described as a rapturous joy or elated energy. It’s more like enthusiasm than happiness. Sometimes it comes as a gentle, uplifting, energizing sensation that is a pleasure to experience with an open heart. At other times, it may manifest as a powerful tingling that sweeps through your body, sending pleasant “chills” up the back of your spine and down the top of your head, bringing tears of joy to your eyes. Piti may also arise as euphoric bliss, or an intense energy that shakes your body and makes your limbs tremble and twitch. This is why piti is also called “rapture.”

Here’s a description of pīti from someone on Reddit:

> I had an experience of strong piti where I was in tears, my body was overcome by a strong buzzing feeling, my whole body was shaking and my breath was rapid and uncontrollable. It was incredibly pleasant, but also kind of overwhelming.

But don’t let that scare you. There are many different ways pīti can appear. Here’s my own description:

> I felt a sense of energy similar to what I experience when I’m excited about something. But this time it wasn’t similar to when I’m excited about something, it _was_ a state of excitement. What I mean by that is that it was a very intense feeling of excitement. It felt like a lot of “buzzing” (in the same way as a weed buzz), but not like when I’m on weed, but more like when I’m really, really excited and buzzing.
> 
> I feel like I’m repeating myself a lot, because I can’t quite find a better way to explain the feeling. I guess the best way to explain it is that it’s a state where you feel very excited, but you don’t know why. The excitement is just there. But you don’t feel anxious, you’re just very excited–like you want to do something, but you don’t know what.
