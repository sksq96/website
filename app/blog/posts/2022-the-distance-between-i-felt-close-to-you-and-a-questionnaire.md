“I felt close to you.” That’s a beautiful sentiment to express to your romantic partner on a given day, perhaps as a way of expressing gratitude for something she did. But, unfortunately, it’s not specific enough to be useful in research.

Instead, you might end up with questions like the following (which I just made up):

> I felt close to my partner today. 1 = not at all, 7 = extremely

and you get responses like

> A: 4, B: 4, C: 4

all of whom will get the same score in the analysis.

What they actually meant is something more like

> A: “I felt similar to my partner.”  
> B: “I felt a lot of warmth toward my partner.”  
> C: “I loved being with my partner.”

These are all different things! They are all dimensions of closeness, along with many others.

So it would be more accurate to have a multidimensional closeness scale:

> (1 = not at all, 7 = extremely)  
> I felt a lot of warmth toward my partner today.
> I felt similar to my partner today.
> I loved being with my partner today.

But this is still problematic. Because we still end up with the same score in the analysis

> A: 1 6 1 = 8  
> B: 6 1 1 = 8  
> C: 1 1 6 = 8

What I actually want, as one researcher and one person who has been in a romantic relationship, is to be able to track the development of my own feelings and beliefs and experiences of closeness over time. I want to see how this fluctuates with my own health, sleep, and mood; with stress at work; with interactions with my partner.

So far, I’ve only a couple of longitudinal studies of couples. They track couples through the day to better understand the dynamics of everyday interactions.

But they don’t measure closeness.

The most relevant paper I’ve found is on the effects of MDMA on couples. They examine the effects of MDMA / 3,4-methylenedioxy-methamphetamine on post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Here is their procedure for the day of the MDMA session:

> The 6-h experimental session will begin between 10:00 am and 11:30 am.… At 1.5 and 4.5 h after first drug administration, participants will be given the second and third doses of drug (the full dose or one-half dose of MDMA, respectively, or a full dose of placebo)

Participants answered the following questions throughout the day, on an hourly basis:

> How are you feeling right now?
> I am feeling close to my partner right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling anxious right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling happy right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling sad right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling relaxed right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling angry right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> I am feeling irritable right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)

After the session, participants were given follow-up surveys:

> 1. How are you feeling right now?
> 2. I am feeling close to my partner right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 3. I am feeling anxious right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 4. I am feeling happy right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 5. I am feeling sad right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 6. I am feeling relaxed right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 7. I am feeling angry right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 8. I am feeling irritable right now. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 9. I felt close to my partner during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 10. I felt anxious during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 11. I felt happy during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 12. I felt sad during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 13. I felt relaxed during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 14. I felt angry during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 15. I felt irritable during the drug session. (1 = not at all; 7 = extremely)
> 16. Please indicate your agreement or disagreement with the following statement: The drug increased my sense of closeness with my partner. (1 = strongly disagree; 7 = strongly agree)

They also measured PTSD:

> We will use the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition (CAPS-5), to measure PTSD symptom severity

Which is important to know, but not relevant to my interests.

They give these as examples of the types of questions they were tracking for the 6 hour session. But only show results for closeness and PTSD.

And this is enough for this particular paper. But it’s not enough for me personally.

I want to know more about the state close scale. I want to know more about the possible mechanisms for why MDMA increases closeness. And how this increases closeness. PTSD and closeness is all I have to go on here. I can make a tentative hypothesis about the mechanisms that related to closeness (increased oxytocin, and so forth). But I can’t say anything about how this increase in closeness might manifest.

I want more questions. I want more data. I want to be able to track my own feelings of closeness in real time, and see how they relate to my own health, mood, and sleep. I want to understand more about the mechanisms and the way closeness is increased. What’s changing? How is closeness manifesting?

How might a researcher do this?

The biggest problem might be that they might not even know what questions to ask! So let’s start with that.

The first thing I’d ask the participants is to clarify what they mean when they say they felt close to their partner during the drug session, or at a specific hour during the session. Ask them what this felt like. What they were thinking about. What they were doing.

Then I’d use their responses to craft closeness questions to use in future experiments.
