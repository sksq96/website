I am reading a lot of interesting things which I am saving in my [notes](https://www.evernote.com/pub/2ed5b8a5-9190-4b10-b1b4-1e0c66e543fc). Here is one of them that really struck me:

> Imagine that while you were depressed, you couldn’t stop watching a show that you really liked. Then you weren’t depressed anymore, but somehow you were still unable to stop watching the show, even though it no longer seemed meaningful or rewarding. You’d still press buttons to open the next episode, but the show itself would have lost its meaning.

What would that be like? What would it be like to not be able to stop watching a show you liked, but without it feeling meaningful or rewarding? Hold on, it gets better:

> Imagine that you were depressed, but for some reason you were still able to do all the things that you normally enjoyed doing. You would hang out with friends, go hiking, play music, etc, but none of it felt meaningful or rewarding, even though you would normally find it very meaningful and rewarding. Imagine that you were able to do these things, but somehow you were unable to care about them.

So I’m thinking about the times in my life where I didn’t care. When I was really in the weeds with depression, I just didn’t care about things. Things lost their meaning. My life was a whirlwind of confusion, misery and fog. There wasn’t any thought about meaning or purpose. I just wanted to survive.

And I have had kinds of depression in the past, but I was still able to do most of the things I wanted to do. I was able to go to class, talk to friends, play games, sleep, and do most of my normal activities. But it all felt hollow. Those activities were all just hollow husks of what used to be colorful and fun.

What would it be like if your life continued to function but you lost the ability to recognize that anything mattered? Imagine doing all the things you do, but somehow losing the sense that any of it is important.

This is the end of this section of the notes and my notes say that I “love” that last sentence. It’s true that I really struck me when I read it.

I still remember a time when I was playing video games, but lost the sense that any of it was important. It wasn’t that I stopped enjoying it. I still enjoyed it, but it lost all sense of importance. I didn’t care about anything that happened in my games. I just played for the sake of playing.

It was a hollow experience, but it was one that I wouldn’t trade.
