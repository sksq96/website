The easiest way to describe Coke Studio is this: it’s an Indian spinoff of MTV Unplugged. India is a culture that doesn’t have a word for music because every word is a form of song. Classical Indian music is complex, with a dozen musical modes (called ragas), some of which require a dozen microtones to hear. The Indian sense of rhythm is similarly layered. Coke Studio emphasizes the distinctive ragas of songs and their authors’ lineage of training.

To start listening to Coke Studio, the best way is to follow a friend’s taste. If you’re reading this, you’re reading a friend’s recommendation. Go ahead and search them up on your music app right now. Coke Studio is a fantastic friend for a rainy day.

Coke Studio’s first season came out in 2008 and it has been releasing content ever since. Since their inception, Coke Studio’s producers have stepped in to produce individual songs. These are listed as ‘Coke Studio Bharat’ rather than seasonal episodes.

The introduction to Coke Studio I suggest for a new listener is this:

1. Start with Husna, the most popular Coke Studio song of 2017.
2. Listen to the Coke Studio songs in this post. (The first Coke Studio I heard was **Chaiyya Chaiyya**. I recommend **Iktara**, **Sayonee**, and **Phir Mohabbat** as well.)
3. Continue with the songs of the Coke Studio episode linked at the end of this post.

**Husna** is a song about a young man in the hills of Kashmir who is being baited by tempting women. The women are witches that are trying to lure the young man to be eaten. Husna (who is a witch) is offering the man a choice of delights. The man is tempted by the choice of options, and he is not sure if he should hold out.

I like this song for the delight in the voices. Pay attention to the female singer’s voice and the backup vocals. The song is a bhajan, a call-and-response set of lyrics often used in devotional music.

**Chaiyya Chaiyya** is an extremely popular Bollywood song. It was featured in a 1998 musical film. The dancers danced on a moving train. The song is a fusion of Sufism and Sufi poetry in the Punjabi language. The song includes the famous line “Bolo bolo bolo re, bolo bolo bolo, bolo bolo bolo, chehra hai ya khulla gulab ka” (“Say say say, is this a face or an open flower”).

I like Coke Studio’s version of the song for its simplicity. I recommend the first two minutes of the song.

**Iktara** is a song about a woman who is seeing and dating a man. She wants to fall in love with a man, but she has a complicated past. She does not know how to act in a relationship, even though she has had years of experience in relationships.

I like Coke Studio’s version of the song for the lyrics. The last verse of the song is the most important part.

**Sayonee** is a song about a young man who has to work a lot. He often has to spend long nights away from home, and he needs to get home. He has to spend time away from his family and friends. He is struggling with the stress of his life.

I like Coke Studio’s version of the song for the soloist. The song is a long one, and the soloist is the only one who can carry the song.

**Phir Mohabbat** is a song about a man who is falling in love with a woman. The man is a soldier who is in the army. He is in love with a woman who is the daughter of a rich landlord. The man is in love with the woman, and he is having a hard time letting go of her.

I like Coke Studio’s version of the song for the soloist. The song is a bhajan, and the soloist is the only one who can carry the song. I recommend the first two minutes of the song.

Coke Studio is a long series with many episodes. I have a lot of favorite episodes, but the ones that I have linked to here are the ones that I liked the most. I hope you enjoy them.
