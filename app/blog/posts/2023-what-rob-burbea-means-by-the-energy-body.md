I’ve been reading Rob Burbea’s book Seeing that Frees, and I came across this passage:

*The second way of letting go of the body (and the self) is the phenomenological sense of the body. Here we encourage the dissolution of the body image. First the body is explored sensing it parts by parts, allowing an understanding that the body is not one but a collection of sensations and perceptions. Then other ways of sensing the body can be explored, including sensing the whole body at once. Here we begin exploring the body as an energy body, seeing the entire body as a field of energy. We sense the energy body directly, allowing our attention to be diffused through the body rather than focused on areas of the body. This allows it to be sensed as a whole, as one field. In this way the body image is dissolved, along with the sense of a self which is felt to be located inside the body image.*

This made me wonder: what does he mean by “energy body”? Can I understand it just as a way of perceiving, without assuming a metaphysical claim?

Let me try to imagine how I’d explain the practice without using the phrase “energy body.” I think I’d say something like:

*One way to let go of the body is to see the “body” as just a bunch of different parts and to see your experience of it as just a bunch of different sensations. It’s a jumble of sensations in constant flux. You don’t have a “body”. You have something that seems to be a body because you’ve learned to attend to certain sensations as a single category, but it’s not really one thing. It’s just a bunch of different sensations you’ve bundled together. And you can learn to not do that.*

*Start by sensing your finger. It feels like something. What does it feel like? Can you sense your finger without thinking about it? You can try noticing the ways that your finger feels like a finger, or you can try making that perception dissolve by noticing the ways that your finger is actually just a lot of different sensations, like the feeling of warmth, the feeling of a pulse, the feeling of a sticky patch, etc.*

*You can also try something different. You can try to become aware of your whole body at once. You can try to have your attention be everywhere at once. Your attention is often focused, but it doesn’t need to be focused. Try noticing things on your left, and on your right, and up and down, and front and back. Let your attention be diffused. See what it’s like to sense your whole body at once.*

*You can also see your body as made out of energy. You can see it as energy. All the sensations that make up your body are like expressions of energy—some of them are sensations of energy, and some of them are sensations of things which are made out of energy. And you can see all the sensations of your body as energy. And you can see your body as one field of energy.*

*You can also see your body as not a body. You can see it as a field of energy, and you can let that field of energy be not a body at all. You can let it be a field of energy. And if you do this, you might find that the self* *dissolves. If you stop seeing your body as a body, you might stop seeing yourself as a self. If you see your body as a field of energy, you might see yourself as a field of energy. And if you let go of the body image, you might let go of the self-image.*

And what do I mean by “energy”? I mean something like “a property that I can sense and that I can see objects as having that property”. I use the word “energy” because I feel it’s the best word we have for this, but I don’t mean it in a scientific sense. I don’t mean that there’s a physical thing called “energy” in the body, or that there’s a metaphysical thing called “energy” in the body. I mean that I can sense this property, and that I can see objects as having this property.

This seems to be a better way of expressing it. I’m not sure I’d even say “energy”, but I think it’s a good word to use because it’s so familiar and because it’s so evocative. But I don’t want to make it sound like I’m referring to a physical thing or a metaphysical thing. I’m referring to a property that I can sense and that I can see objects as having that property.
