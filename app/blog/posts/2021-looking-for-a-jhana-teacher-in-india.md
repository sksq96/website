One of the more interesting things that happened over my time in India is that I started getting interested in jhana and vipassana meditation.

It was all the way in December 2019, but I do remember that there was an article in Aeon on the experience of jhana meditation that fascinated me, and I was curious to learn more about it.

I had been a meditator in the past, having done a half-hearted 10-day Vipassana retreat in 2016, and having done various levels of meditation through Headspace and can now sit quietly for half an hour and focus on my breathing, but that's about it.

The Aeon article was the first time that I realised that the Buddhist tradition had a very detailed taxonomy of meditative states, and an algorithm for getting to them. It also talks about how Buddha and his disciples were apparently able to reach these states in a few hours, and a few minutes, when required.

I was also fascinated by the descriptions of the states themselves. These states are referred to as jhanas in the Theravada Buddhist tradition, and they are described as follows:

The first jhana is a pleasant, non-sensual, blissful mental state that arises when the mind has been freed from five mental hindrances: desire/lust, aversion/hatred, sloth/laziness, restlessness/agitation, and doubt. It is accompanied by a mental quality akin to joy or rapture.

The second jhana is a deeper, more pleasant mental state that arises when you let go of the discursive thinking that characterised the first jhana. It is accompanied by a mental quality akin to joy or rapture.

The third jhana is a deeper, more pleasant mental state that arises when you let go of the rapture that characterised the second jhana. It is accompanied by a mental quality akin to happiness, well-being, or contentment.

The fourth jhana is a deeper, more pleasant mental state that arises when you let go of the happiness that characterised the third jhana. It is accompanied by a mental quality akin to equanimity or peacefulness.

There are more jhanas, but we can skip them for now.

#### Finding a teacher

So, I was in India and I figured that was as good a place as any to look for a jhana teacher, and I got to work.

The first time I looked was on Google, and I couldn't find anyone good. I came across a few teachers who mostly seemed to be doing it as a side gig.

Some weeks later, I was talking to my friend Siddharth at a cafe, and we got to talking about jhana. He was like, "It's so easy! You just need to let go of the hindrances!". I was like, "Oh yeah, I've read the instructions, but I don't think I can do it without a teacher."

He was like, "I don't think you need a teacher. You just need to practice and be regular about it."

I was like, "Okay, yeah, that's true, but I think it's easier with a teacher, and I'm here in India, so I might as well find a teacher."

He was like, "Yeah, I guess you're right."

And then he opened up Google on his phone and started looking for teachers.

So, we did find one, and we called him up, and he was like, "Yeah, I can teach you jhanas, but I'm here in Mumbai, so you'll have to come here."

I was like, "Oh, that's not possible for me right now. I'm here in Hyderabad, and I don't know when I'll be able to come to Mumbai."

He was like, "Okay, that's fine. I can teach you online, but I'll need to see you on video."

I was like, "That's okay, but I don't think I'll be able to do it regularly. I'm here in India, and I'm not sure when I'll be able to do it."

He was like, "Okay, that's fine. I can teach you online, but I'll need to see you on video."

I was like, "Okay, that's fine. I'll do it."

So, I got the information and then when I got home, I looked up the name of the teacher and found that he was a bit shady, and other people had had bad experiences with him (he behaved inappropriately with women). I was like, "Oh, that's not good. I don't want to do that."

So, I decided to look for another teacher, and I found one, and I called him up, and he was like, "Yeah, I can teach you jhanas, but I'm here in Mumbai, so you'll have to come here."

I was like, "Okay, that's fine. I'll do it."

So, I got the information, and then when I got home, I looked up the name of the teacher and found that he was a bit shady, and other people had had bad experiences with him. I was like, "Oh, that's not good. I don't want to do that."

In the end, I never got around to finding a teacher. I also looked into meditation retreats, but I didn't find any that were focused on jhana.

I still have the tab open from back when I was doing research. I find myself looking at it sometimes, but I never got around to doing it.
