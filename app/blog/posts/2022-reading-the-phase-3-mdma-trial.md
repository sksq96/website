## The two arms

This was a trial of patients with moderate to severe PTSD, as measured by the CAPS-5 scale. The CAPS-5 is a clinician-rated scale, where the clinician talks with the patient about specific symptoms and asks them to rate severity and frequency, as well as distress and how well they're doing in their life.

There were two arms in this trial: MDMA-assisted therapy, and placebo- (inert substance-) assisted therapy. You may think, "That's impossible! MDMA is a powerful drug, and everyone knows what it feels like!" And indeed, much of the discussion around this trial has focused on the apparent failure of blinding; patients and clinicians alike were often able to guess which arm a patient had been assigned to.

But blinding is important to the placebo effect. If you think you've taken MDMA, it might have a different effect on you than if you think you've taken a placebo. So the researchers went to great lengths to try to blind people.

First off, they used active placebos. The placebo group wasn't given sugar pills; they were given an "inactive substance with perceptible effects". Some people got intranasal saline, and others got niacin.

Niacin causes a "niacin flush", which causes some vasodilation, redness, and itching. It's a pretty good candidate for an active placebo! Neither saline nor niacin induce euphoria, mood changes, or psychedelic effects like MDMA does, but they both cause bodily sensations that could be confused for MDMA.

The researchers also used blinding checks for the patients and the clinicians. After every MDMA session, they asked patients and clinicians to guess which arm they were in and to rate their confidence about that guess.

## The primary endpoint

The primary endpoint of the study was change in CAPS-5 score from baseline to 18 weeks. They measured this using a linear mixed effects model, which is a way of dealing with repeated measures of the same person. (You could just average everyone's CAPS-5 score and then take the difference after 18 weeks, but this is a better way.)

The researchers found that the MDMA group had a 24.4 point decrease in CAPS-5 score, while the placebo group had a 13.9 point decrease. This was a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001). The effect was large: cohen's d was 0.91.

They also looked at functional impairment with the Sheehan Disability Scale, and found that the MDMA group had a 23 point reduction, while the placebo group had a 14.8 point reduction (p = 0.03; d = 0.43).

85% of the MDMA group had clinically significant improvement in CAPS-5 score (defined as a 10 point drop), while only 62% of the placebo group did (p = 0.023). 67% of the MDMA group no longer had PTSD at the end of the trial, compared to 32% of the placebo group (p = 0.0002).
