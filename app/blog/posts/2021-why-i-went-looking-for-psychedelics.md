This is a post about my experience with magic mushrooms. I decided to write this post because I've found it difficult to find first-hand experiences with psychedelics online. I'll talk about 3 main points:

1. My journey leading up to magic mushrooms.
2. What it was like when I was "tripping" on mushrooms.
3. My lessons from the experience.

## How I got into magic mushrooms
I got interested in magic mushrooms from the book "How to change your mind" from Michael Pollan. This book was recommended to me by Astral Codex Ten's Scott Alexander. I started reading Slate Star Codex (Now Astral Codex Ten) in April 2019. It is a blog by Scott Alexander, who writes about a wide variety of topics ranging from politics, health, rationality, and AI.

I was a moderator on the Slate Star Codex subreddit during that time. I read SSC every Sunday for about a year. I think that is when I started to read about and got introduced to some ideas around psychedelics.

One of the first articles I read was Scott's anecdotes with magic mushrooms. I remember being fascinated by the idea that there were substances that could induce a feeling of "oneness" with the universe, and that it could possibly have a meaningful impact on one's life.

From my understanding of the literature, magic mushrooms are widely believed to be a good treatment for depression. The belief is that if you don't know how to meditate, magic mushrooms are a cheap and safe way to have a "mystical experience".

I think that's when I became interested in trying psychedelics. I was quite depressed during that time (and still am), and I wanted a magic cure to make me happy. I didn't want to spend my life looking at life through the lens of cynicism. I wanted to be happy and enjoy life. I was also interested in the promise that psychedelics could lead to a mystical experience, which would make me feel closer to God.

## My experience with magic mushrooms
I went to NYC for New Year's this year and ended up taking 1.5g of mushrooms. I think this was a fairly amount for a first-time user. I was with my friend and two other friends from NYU. I had a very pleasant experience. I think the biggest effect I noticed was that I became much more sensitive to color and sounds.

I had this experience where I saw a construction worker walking with a hi-vis vest and I couldn't stop staring at it. It was like my brain was a camera and I was turning up the saturation and contrast. It was unnerving, but the colors were also very pleasant. I also felt like my visual experience was heightened.

I experienced the same thing with sound. I remember listening to music, and noticing how it felt when the musical notes changed. It felt like my brain was understanding things too well.

One other thing I noticed was that when I was sitting with my friends and had some food, I felt very uncomfortable. It was one of the weirdest feelings I felt. It felt like my brain was too aware that I was putting food in my mouth, and chewing and swallowing it slowly. From what I understand, this is a common experience with people who try psychedelics.

## The bigger question
Overall, this was a very pleasant experience for me, but I don't think I'd do it again. I found it quite distracting and I don't know if it had any big impact on my life. I think in general, I consider myself a fairly cautious person and I try to control things, so psychedelics are something that I'd probably stay away from, but I'm open to trying micro-dosing.

The biggest thing I'm taking away from this experience is that one shouldn't have psychedelics with the hope of getting something from it. I think that's a good way to get disappointed. For most people, I think it's a very pleasant experience, but it might be a bit overwhelming for some people. I think the bigger question to ask is: "If you aren't enjoying the small things in life, why do you think it will change if you have psychedelics?"

I suppose it could help if you are depressed because it might help you see the world from a different perspective. If you are someone who feels jaded or cynical about the world, I think trying psychedelics in a safe environment with friends could have a big impact on your life, but I don't know if it would be the right choice for everyone.
