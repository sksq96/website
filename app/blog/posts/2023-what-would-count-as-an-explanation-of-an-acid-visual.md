In a previous post I listed out a bunch of things I don’t understand about psychedelics. Here, I’d like to start with one specific visual phenomenon that piqued my curiosity and attempt to explain it.

I want to approach this explanation the way David Deutsch describes it in his book The Beginning of Infinity.

So our starting point will be a phenomenon that I can describe accurately.

# The phenomenon:

I’ll describe the phenomenon in two ways. The first is from my own experience and the second is a handy illustration created by a redditor named “3xp0”.

The way I remember experiencing this phenomenon is that I was noticing the beautiful, colorful patterns on my bedsheet when I suddenly noticed that the pattern was changing with every blink. This would have been normal enough, but what was astonishing was that the sheet remained a simple plain color when my eyes were open and the pattern only showed up when I closed my eyes. I was astonished that the pattern was changing with every blink and was further astonished that the pattern would vanish when I opened my eyes.

Here’s the illustration I found on reddit.

![image](https://i.imgur.com/tVm4ryz.jpg)

# Explanation:

I am going to describe the same phenomenon in two ways because that’s what David Deutsch does in this section. The first description is the accurate one. The second is a mathematical pattern that is somehow reminiscent of this visual phenomenon. The idea is that when you hear the mathematical description, you’ll think “Oh yeah, it’s similar to what I’m seeing every time I blink”. And then you can connect the mathematical description with the accurate description in your mind.

Let’s start with the accurate description. When you look at an object, light reflected from the object travels through your retina to your brain. The image you see is created when your brain processes this information. This makes it possible for you to “see” things even when nothing is directly in front of you, as long as your brain gets the right impulses. Which is why you can “see” hallucinations even though there’s nothing in front of you. Hallucinations are sensations that your brain processes, even though the information doesn’t originate from your retina.

If you close your eyes, you can still imagine things. So when you see a pattern when you close your eyes, it’s also like your imagination creating a pattern. At this point you might be wondering “But this is not imagination. I don’t have control over this pattern and it’s not like I’m consciously fabricating it.” But this is actually an example of unconscious imagination.

Your imagination is the result of your brain processing all the information it has access to. This includes information about how you see things and whatever other information your brain has access to. This includes unconscious information.

When you blink, the information changes. The lens in your eye adjusts to focus on your eyelid. And the new visual information that reaches your brain is different from the information that was reaching your brain before.This means that what your brain is processing has changed. The image you would create when you close your eyes would be different from the image you would create when you are looking at something. And because this information is processed by your brain and your imagination is an output of your brain, your imagination will also change. Which is why the pattern you see changes when you blink.

I call this an “explanation” because now you know why you see a different pattern every time you blink.

Now for the mathematical description. I found the following quote from Conway’s book On Numbers and Games.

> Conway’s oft-quoted mantra is:“Find simple solutions to the simplest nontrivial problems, and then only generalize at the very last step.”

The simple but nontrivial problem here is the Collatz Conjecture.

Here’s a description I found on Wikipedia.

> If the number is even divide it by 2. If the number is odd multiply it by 3 and add 1.

Conway created a structure called “The Collatz Tree” for this conjecture.

![image](https://i.imgur.com/Vy0FHDk.png)

This looks a lot like the pattern I saw when I blinked.

Now let’s connect this mathematical description with the accurate description. The pattern is generated by simple operations that are repeated a bunch of times. Similarly, I think, the pattern you see is generated by simple, unconscious operations that are repeated a bunch of times. This is a possible explanation for why the pattern changes every time you blink.

This is a good start for an explanation, and it’s probably incomplete.

I’d like to look at other visual phenomena and see if this explanation can explain them too.

I found the following visual phenomenon on reddit.

> I found out I can make crazy patterns with my eyes closed and breathe in deeply

It looks like this:

![image](https://i.imgur.com/P1wH5yf.gif)

Let’s see if we can explain this with the explanation we used for the previous phenomenon.

The pattern changes because your brain is processing different information. When you breathe in, your brain gets different information than when you breathe out. Which is why when you breathe in, you see a different pattern.

It looks like our explanation is working.

Let’s look at another phenomenon. I found this one on reddit as well.

> My eyes are tripping on their own

![image](https://i.imgur.com/FiUjD8v.gif)

This is probably a difficult one to explain. Let’s try.

Remember, your imagination is an output of your brain processing information it has access to.

The simplest way to explain what’s going on in the image is to say that your brain is processing information it has access to and that information is causing your imagination to create this pattern.

But we’d like to know why your imagination is creating this pattern.

What information could your brain be processing that could explain this pattern?

This is a hard one. I don’t know the answer yet.

I think I’ll stop here. There are other visual phenomena I’d like to explain. But this is a good start.
