I was at a conference at NYU in February 2019 when I saw OpenAI's blog post. I was, like many others, impressed.

The text samples they posted on their site were quite interesting.


I remember I was in awe looking at the generated story about unicorns.

It was 4 am and I was a bit sleepy but that was still very cool.

That's when I asked myself:

> *What makes GPT-2 different from other models?*

While many people were talking about the language model GPT-2, I personally think the underlying architecture GPT is very important and the current GPT-2 is an incremental improvement. I still find it amazing they could produce a model that can write so well  by just doing "next word prediction". That's the power of scale! (more data and higher number of parameters = better model). The only way to make this model better is to feed it with more and higher quality data, and also increase the number of parameters.

After GPT-2 was released, many people were arguing against their decision of not releasing the weights. It was almost like the rise of the data/model censorship. How do we know when we should censor the model and when not? How do we know that it's actually dangerous to release the model? Why are we (not) letting the community decide for themselves? There were many interesting discussions online on this matter.

I tried fine-tuning the model (the small version) in late spring 2019. It was fun to read their code and replicate the fine-tuning process. I wrote a Medium article on how to do it.

I also fine-tuned the model on Reddit data (first on data from a Reddit thread and then on data from Reddit's /r/AskReddit). I was surprised by how good the results were. I tried to add the model to my Facebook chatbot but it was way too slow. I ended up making the chatbot output a random pregenerated comment. The chatbot was just a side project.

I was really in love with the model. It was almost as if I found a new love after RL and it was replacing RL. I was drawn to language models and away from RL. (I still find RL fun but I'm more into language models now.)

It was the end of May 2019 and GPT-2 still hasn't ceased to blow my mind. (and probably it never will)
