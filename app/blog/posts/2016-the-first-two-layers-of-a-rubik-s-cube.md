17 Dec 2016

I've been trying to learn to solve a Rubik's cube for a while now. My starting point was this video by badmephisto. I've had enough success with it to give me a sense of accomplishment, but also enough failure that I'm dissatisfied. I want to learn to solve it, but I also want to do it the right way. Something about the badmephisto method is so inelegant that I feel like I'm cheating if I solve the cube this way.

Last night, after about three tries that ended in failure, I decided to try to gather some more information. I searched "Rubik's cube f2l" and "Rubik's cube first two layers", and I found this video by YesNoMaybeSometimes. I felt like the author was getting at something that I already knew but that I couldn't articulate, so I watched the video several times. And I finally got it.

In the badmephisto video, he says that the goal is to insert an "edge-corner pair" into a slot. I never really understood what that meant, so I watched the video several more times. But I still didn't understand. It was only after watching YesNoMaybeSometimes' video that I realized that he was sticking the edge and the corner together, and then moving them together as a unit!

Let me explain. Here is a Rubik's cube that I've scrambled. I want to solve the white and green corner in the top right:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-1.jpg)

If I were to solve it badmephisto's way, I would somehow get the white-green edge into the top right slot of the first two layers. But if I look at this cube, I'll notice that the white-green-red corner is in the bottom left slot in the top layer. So I'll do this:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-2.jpg)

So now the white-green edge is in the right slot! All I need to do is to get the white-green-red corner into that slot. If I look at the cube, I'll see that the white-green-red corner is in the bottom left slot in the first two layers. So I'll do this:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-3.jpg)

Now I'm stuck. If I perform the first step again, that will undo the second step. If I perform the second step again, that will undo the first step.

But if I look at the cube again, I'll see that there is another white-green edge in the bottom right slot in the first two layers! So I'll repeat the first step (and only the first step):

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-4.jpg)

Now I'm stuck again, but this time in a different way. If I repeat the second step, that will undo the first step. But if I look at the cube, I'll see that there is another white-green-red corner in the bottom right slot in the first two layers! So I'll repeat the second step (and only the second step):

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-5.jpg)

After that, I won't be stuck. I can repeat the first step to finish solving this slot. But I feel like this is way too much work, and it also gets harder if I try to do this with other slots. But I didn't know what to do until I watched the YesNoMaybeSometimes video.

So here's the way I would now solve the white-green-red corner and the white-green edge:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-6.jpg)

Notice that I've rotated the cube so that the corner and the edge are in a more convenient position. Now I'll get the corner and the edge to stick together:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-7.jpg)

And now I'll move that pair of pieces to the correct slot:

![](/blog/rubiks-cube-8.jpg)

Done! If you're wondering why I did those moves, then I'll explain: I tried to get the pieces to stick together. When I got them to stick together, I thought about what I wanted to do next (move them to the correct slot) and tried to do that. So this way of solving the first two layers isn't just faster, it's also more intuitive.

So I'm currently going back through the videos that I've saved. As I go through them, I'll try to find moves or combinations of moves that I don't understand. And if I find one, I'll post another blog post explaining it.
