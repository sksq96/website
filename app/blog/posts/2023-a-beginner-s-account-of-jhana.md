*Today I recount my first experience with jhanas, one year ago*

I began to read about jhanas a little over a year ago. At the time I had no idea what the jhanas were, and within several weeks I had a sloppy account of my first jhana experience. I'm now a little bit more familiar with the jhanas, and my present understanding suggests that my experience was a shallow jhana 1. But though I now know jhanas a bit better than I did a year ago, I'm still a beginner. So I recount my first jhana experience as a beginner.

<hr>

This first account is what I wrote in December.

*With the benefit of hindsight, I think these were jhana-like states. I’m not sure if it was even jhana 1. But it was very pleasurable, and the amount of time between re-entering the state and the state’s peak grew smaller and smaller. So it became easier to revisit the state and get into it.*

*I think it happened because of the book I was reading, Into Thin Air, Jon Krakauer’s account of a group of Everest climbers, many of whom died. Something about the book made it very difficult to concentrate. I was reading it on my phone, and I kept wanting to go on my phone and do something else. But I was determined to concentrate on it.*

*I noticed that I really enjoyed finally focusing on the book, and that after a while I would start to really enjoy myself, and then I would get anxious and distracted. I remember this becoming a kind of cycle.*

*I also remember being able to re-enter the state, but much later, maybe a week later, and it was much harder to do.*

<hr>

And here is my second account, a little more articulate:

*I remember when I was reading Into Thin Air, a book about the Everest disaster, and I was reading it on my phone. And I remember being in a state of great bliss. I wasn’t sure how to identify it. I was just so happy reading, and I was doing something I had been doing for a while – reading, which was something I always enjoyed. And the state extended to when I would do something else like eat or talk to a friend.*

*I’d just be filled with bliss, and if I annoyed myself in some way, I could reverse it by reading a book and getting back into the state.*

*I think the state came because I was determined to concentrate on a book that had been difficult for me to concentrate on. I kept being distracted because I was reading it on my phone and I would get bored. I would want to check something else. But I just kept forcing myself to concentrate on the book. I kept getting distracted once I started to get to the peak of the state, but I just kept forcing myself to go back. Each time I got distracted, it would take less time to return to the peak of the bliss.*

<hr>

I have a few questions for the interested teacher who might want to help me understand if this was a proper jhana experience. First, the discourse on jhanas I'm familiar with suggests that a jhana is an absorption. Often the teachers talk of being absorbed into the object. Is the reading of a book a good object of absorption? Second, I was able to get into the absorption several times, but, as far as I can tell, I wasn't able to re-enter it after several weeks. I wasn't trying to do so. In the beginning, I was just aware of how pleasurable the reading of the book had become. What is it about my experience that might have made it a jhana? Third, how do I get back? I don't know.
