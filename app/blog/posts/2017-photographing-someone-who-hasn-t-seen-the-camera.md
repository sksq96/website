As I was reading an article on photography and the role of the photographer, I was reminded of this question I’d looked up on Google some time back. I think the question was phrased something like: “photography without the person knowing”. Here is a screenshot of the search I made in October.

![Google search screen shot](/assets/images/2017/11/Screen-Shot-2017-11-11-at-4.12.25-PM.png)


I was thinking about this question for a few days after making the search, and started writing this post. As I read the article, I found that the question was being discussed there itself.

The question is about photography, specifically candid photography. The question is about when the photographer takes a photo of you, and you are not aware of it. Such a photo is a candid photo because you are not aware that someone is taking a photo of you and so you do not try to pose for the photo. I think these photos are interesting because they capture a “natural” version of you, instead of a “posed” version of you.

But I am not looking at this question just from the perspective of photography, but also from the perspective of what I expect of the photographer. Basically, how do I want the photographer to behave with me?

To explore this question, let me set up a hypothetical situation. Let’s say I am walking on the street, and a photographer is walking towards me, and they want to take a photo of me (photo 1). Now there are two cases that can play out. In one case, I see them taking the photo and so I pose for it (photo 2). In the second case, I don’t see them taking the photo and so I don’t pose for it (photo 3).

![Street scene](/assets/images/2017/11/Screen-Shot-2017-11-11-at-4.34.13-PM.png)

Both photos are taken at the same time. But I’d prefer that the photographer held on to the first photo and deleted the second one. Why so? Because I think the first photo is a better photo of me than the second one. If I’m not aware that a photographer is taking a photo of me, then their taking the photo is more or less like them spying on me. And I would not want that.

Basically, I would like photographers to only take photos of me that I am aware of. I would like photographers to not take photos of me without my knowledge. I think this would make me more comfortable around them.

However, this definition seems to have a problem. There are many situations where I do not know that someone is taking a photo of me, but I would not mind if they shared that photo. One situation that comes to mind is security cameras. I don’t mind if they capture my photo. I think in these situations, I can see why the photos are being taken, and I think it is good that these photos are being taken.

So an alternate way of framing this would be to ask what would make me comfortable around photographers. I think I would be most comfortable around photographers if I could see their camera while they are taking my photo. It’s not that I want to pose for the photo, it’s just that I want to be aware of the photo being taken. This would make me comfortable around them and I would not feel like they are spying on me.

So the definition I am trying to give here is of what would make me comfortable around photographers. I think this definition can be used to improve photography in public spaces.

![Street scene](/assets/images/2017/11/Screen-Shot-2017-11-11-at-4.51.51-PM.png)

Coming back to the article on photography, I think the question of photography without the person knowing is an important one. I think that photographers should not keep photos of me if I am not aware of them. However, I would not mind if I could see their camera while they were taking my photo. I think this would make me more comfortable around them.

The same question is being discussed in the comments section of the article, and you can see my comment there. I’m linking to it here so that you can see what others are saying about this.

I think this question is worth exploring, and I would like to explore it further. If you have any thoughts on this, please leave them in the comments section.
