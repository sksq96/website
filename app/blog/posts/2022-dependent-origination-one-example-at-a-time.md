I recently watched a talk by Ven. Thanissaro Bhikkhu on dependent origination. A monk asked him how to teach dependent origination to others. Ven. Thanissaro responded with a few words I wrote down: “point to examples”.

The next day, I went to a meditation session by John Brasington. I was already familiar with him because I had saved this talk in October 2021; I watched it and I loved it, but I didn’t really listen to it. It was a very calm voice that embodied the meditation his words were describing.

In any case, I walked into the shrine room and I saw Brasington. He was sitting down and he was wearing a brown robe. The contrast between his gray hair and his brown robe was pleasant to look at. I sat down, he gave us the familiar instructions, and I started meditating. As usual, my attention moved from my breath to the sound of rain to the feeling in my body.

Then, suddenly, I recognized an instance of dependent origination. The rain started on a pleasant note—it made me enjoy the session. But there was a moment when I switched from enjoying the sound to wanting the sound to continue. That’s when I recognized dependent origination. I was like, “Oh, this is contact, this is feeling, this is craving.”

I had heard and read about dependent origination before, but this was the first time I had recognized it. My whole body felt like the joy it felt when I was making progress on that paper two years ago.

Brasington started by saying that dependent origination is the earliest teaching. I researched it today, and I found a sutta in which the Buddha says:

> Now whoever sees Dependent Origination sees Dhamma; whoever sees Dhamma sees Dependent Origination.

I also found here a list of the twelve links:

1. Ignorance: ignorance of dependent origination
2. Formations: the assorted mental states, wholesome and unwholesome
3. Consciousness: like the eye consciousness, ear consciousness, etc.
4. Name and form: namarupa. I think this means something like, recognizing a newly formed object as something I have a name for
5. Six sense bases: the senses
6. Contact: when the senses meet an object, like the eyes meet a visual object
7. Feeling: the valenced sensation (pleasant, unpleasant, neutral)
8. Craving: desiring
9. Clinging: attachment, like I had a craving but now I’m attached to whatever thing I craved
10. Becoming: actually becoming whatever it was that I craved
11. Birth: birth
12. Aging and death: old age and death

From my notes, I think the link I broke was between feeling and craving. I heard the rain, I enjoyed the sound, I wanted the sound to continue, and then I realized that I was in the middle of a dependent origination sequence. But I had the presence of mind to not cling onto the sound. I could have been like, “I like this sound, I wish the sound continued, cling to it,” but I didn’t. I was like, “I like this sound, I wish the sound continued, cling to it,” and then I was like, “Oh, I don’t want to cling onto the sound.” I like the sound, and I wanted it to continue, but I knew that if I went through with the clinging, I wouldn’t be content.

That’s what I think was happening. Not only was I recognizing the links as they were occurring, but I also stopped the chain from progressing. I stopped the clinging from becoming becoming. I stopped clinging from becoming attachment. I stopped clinging from becoming desire. I stopped clinging, period.

I think that’s what I did. And what I felt was joy. I felt a sense of freedom and victory. But that feeling faded. The sound of rain continued, and I continued to enjoy it. But I wasn't in a constant state of joy the same way I was when I was making progress on my paper.

I think that’s because I think I’m thinking too much.
