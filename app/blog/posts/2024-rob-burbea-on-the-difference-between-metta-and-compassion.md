In this post, I want to highlight the difference between metta and compassion. I would like to do that by giving two examples of each of them.

To do this well, let me first listen back to Rob Burbea on the difference between metta and compassion.

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/oAuITgR6qjI?start=3902" height="500" width="100%">

Here are the key parts:

- **Mettā**: a sense of “warmth” and “openness” that wants to bring happiness to others.
- **Compassion**: a sense of “warmth” and “openness” that wants to relieve suffering in others.

Now, I am going to choose a particular way of practicing the two, namely the “Wishing them well” way for metta, and the “Ask them questions” way for compassion.

Let’s imagine that we are with somebody who is having a hard time (last night they slept poorly, and today they are feeling exhausted and irritated).

We are going to do two things:

- Send them good wishes.
- Ask them questions.

Normally, when we send them good wishes, we are practicing metta. Normally, when we ask them questions, we are practicing compassion.

In fact, it is possible to practice metta when you ask them questions and compassion when you send them good wishes. The difference is the intention behind the action.

In the case of metta, you are asking them questions with the intention that their life goes well. In the case of compassion, you are sending good wishes with the intention that their suffering be relieved.

This should make the difference between metta and compassion more clear.

In a nutshell, if you are interacting with somebody who is having a hard time, and you act out of metta, you have the intention of their life going well. If you are interacting with somebody who is having a hard time, and you act out of compassion, you have the intention of their suffering being relieved.
