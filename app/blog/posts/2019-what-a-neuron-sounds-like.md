I've just been mashing the replay button on [Sound of Neurons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-Idp_F6OJ8) while letting the visualization move from the top to the bottom of the neuron. I made a mental note when I found it to revisit it later, and I now realize that this is a good time to do that.

Here's the video again, but with a black box covering up the visualization.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bu5CMuwgz1A

My first reaction to this is that it obviously sounds different from any recording of the action of a single neuron that I've ever heard.

[Electrical Noise in the Brain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZeHge1y0kQ) demonstrates what a single neuron sounds like when you're continually picking up action potentials. You can hear the rise in firing rate when the neuron is stimulated.

[Sounds of the Hippocampus](https://youtu.be/D5vutDIyJ8o) demonstrates the phenomenon where a neuron can fire in "bursts" of multiple action potentials in rapid succession.

What you get from listening to those recordings is that some neurons are more active than others, and that the activity of a neuron isn't always a simple rate. Though the neuron in the first video is noisy, its firing rate is clearly changing due to the stimulus. And the neuron in the second video demonstrates that a neuron's activity isn't always accurately modeled by a single rate.

What strikes me about the Sound of Neurons is that it sounds like a continuous sound that changes pitch in response to some measure of neuron activity. So I hear a difference between activity levels, but I don't hear individual action potentials.

I wonder how the sound was generated.

As it turns out, my wonderings are covered in this companion video: [making the sound of neurons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CoA4L8mV9iA).

and it sounds like the sound changes are generated by a sinusoid where the frequency is a function of the neuron's membrane potential.

And the neuron appears to be modeled with a continuous-time dynamical system that looks a lot like the Lorenz system.

One thing that I notice that I'd overlooked before is that the sound has a "beating" quality to it. That's an artifact of the fact that the sound is generated by multiple sinusoids that are very close in frequency.

Why is that? Well, it sounds like the sound is generated from the activity of a small population of neurons, rather than just a single neuron.

It's cool to dive into this a little bit, to look at the source code and see how it works, and I encourage you to do the same. Seriously, go read the rest of the comments on that video.
