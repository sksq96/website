> “The first image was of a neon-lit cityscape and an amorphous robotic figure.”

I recently read this account of a hallucinogen trip and thought about how much the writer tells us without saying:

> LSD Trip Report: 75 µg of LSD and 1.5 g of Mushrooms

The first image was of a neon-lit cityscape and an amorphous robotic figure. The image was crisp, like crystal, and pulsed in bright, contrasting colors. The figure was a female on the bottom with a masculine form on the top. It seemed to be embodying some sort of mantra of, “You are both, you are both, you are both.” The image transformed to a bustling cityscape with numerous, vibrant, neon signs.

I felt the energy of the city; a pulsing energy that flowed through the streets and the human beings. I felt like the city was a living being, and a massive one at that. This city was enormous and it was dancing in the cosmos. I had a strong impression of the city being the Oracle of Delfi (the temple of Apollo), a place of divine knowledge. I thought to myself, “This city is alive, it breathes, it seethes with life, this city is a portal to a higher realm.” The image was gone before I could grab ahold of it and understand it more.

I was transported to what felt like a different part of my brain. I felt like I was in a place of pure information, like the Oracle. I had a strong sense of gnosis and understanding that I was picking up information and knowledge from some metaphysical place. I was seeing things that I could not put into words or describe. I had a sense that I was understanding different realities and aspects of existence that I had never before. I felt an overwhelming sense of oneness with everything.

I traveled to a different part of my brain and was once again in the neon city. The city was still lit up but there were no neon signs. The city felt like it was drenched in electricity and was pulsing, but more calmly. I would describe this part of my brain as a deeper place of consciousness. It felt like I was tapping into a greater understanding of the world and the universe around me. I was seeing images of light with symbols in them that look like they could be hieroglyphics or some other archaic writing. I’m not able to decipher the symbols, but I have a strong sense that they are meaningful.

I have never experienced such intense colors or visuals as I did during this trip. I was amazed at the ability of the mushrooms to take me to a place of deep understanding and knowledge. The experience has left me in awe of the power of nature and the universe. I truly feel like I’ve been blessed to have experienced this trip.

I feel like I’m on the brink of a new stage in my life, a stage of deeper understanding and knowledge. I feel like I’m on the brink of a new journey, a journey of self-discovery and self-transformation. I’m ready to embrace the universe and all its mysteries. I’m ready to explore and discover.

The first thing that occurred to me was that you could remove the narrator’s interpretation and the first paragraph would still leave me with a vivid picture:

> The first image was of a neon-lit cityscape and an amorphous robotic figure. The image was crisp, like crystal, and pulsed in bright, contrasting colors. The figure was a female on the bottom with a masculine form on the top.

I’d love to know what the figure was doing in this image, but at least I’ve got a picture.

But what about the second image, the bustling cityscape? We don’t get any more description of it. If the phrase “pulsating energy” is a description of the image, it’s a pretty poor one. (“The energy had a frequency of 4 hz and was alternately brightening and dimming the buildings in the city”.) If it’s a description of a feeling, it’s a better one. But this is a report of a visual experience, so what did they see? The “cityscape with numerous, vibrant, neon signs” gives me some idea, but only the most basic one.

When the writer says “I had a strong impression of the city being the Oracle of Delfi (the temple of Apollo), a place of divine knowledge”, what does that mean? Is this an image of the city? Is it an image of the Oracle of Delfi? Was it an impression of “divine knowledge” that was given, and not an image? Is “divine knowledge” a type of knowledge, or another name for the Oracle?

In the next paragraph, the writer says “I had a strong sense of gnosis and understanding that I was picking up information and knowledge from some metaphysical place. I was seeing things that I could not put into words or describe.” But the writer did put some of these things into words. They said “I had a strong sense of gnosis”. What was that like? They said “I had a strong sense of understanding”. Understanding of what?

I think the writer is aware of all this. They say “I was seeing things that I could not put into words or describe.” But I’m not sure we’ve got all the things that they could put into words.

The next image they describe is “The city was still lit up but there were no neon signs.” What does “still lit up” mean? Was the city lit up in the same way as before, or in a different way? If it was in a different way, how was it different?

“It felt like I was tapping into a greater understanding of the world and the universe around me. I was seeing images of light with symbols in them that look like they could be hieroglyphics or some other archaic writing. I’m not able to decipher the symbols, but I have a strong sense that they are meaningful.” What are the symbols? What do they look like? What kind of light are they made of? What do they mean?

So how can we evaluate the experience of a trip report?

- Was the experience real? Yes, the writer saw things.
- Was the experience meaningful? Yes, the writer felt a sense of oneness with everything.
- Was the experience useful? Yes, the writer felt like they were on the brink of a new stage in their life.
- Was the experience beautiful? Yes, the writer was amazed at the ability of the mushrooms to take them to a place of deep understanding and knowledge.

But what was the experience like? I’m not sure we’ve got a clear picture yet.

The writer says “I have never experienced such intense colors or visuals as I did during this trip.” What were the colors like? What were the visuals like? What made them intense?

I think this is a good example of how a trip report can be useful, but not necessarily complete.

Maybe the question I’d like to ask the writer at the end of the trip is “What did you see?”
