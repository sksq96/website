# Key-Value Pairs

Suppose I asked you whether you know someone named “Elizabeth”. You might remember a few people you know with that name. But when I tell you the full name “Elizabeth Bennet”, you immediately think of a few Jane Austen novels.

There are a lot of Elizabethes in the world, and you know a lot of people. With the initial “Elizabeth”, you need to think of a lot of people named “Elizabeth”. It takes a lot of mental effort. When I say “Elizabeth Bennet”, your mind immediately switches to a (more) specific place.

The “key” is the full name “Elizabeth Bennet”, the “value” is your specific memory of the character.

If you met a few people named “Elizabeth Bennet”, then the full name would still be a lot of people. You need more information. Maybe it’s a matter of context or geography. When I say “Elizabeth Bennet from Pride and Prejudice”, you know exactly which book and which character I’m talking about.

The idea of a Key-Value Pair is that you have a collection of things, like a dictionary or a phonebook. You need to find something inside the collection. You don’t know exactly which thing you want, but you know a description of the thing you want. That’s the “Key”. You use the description to find the thing you want. That’s the “Value”.

# Memories

Memories aren’t facts, they are not static store records. Memories are reconstructions. Your brain doesn’t have a collection of recordings of your memories. When you remember something, your brain actively re-creates the memory.

A key aspect of memories is that they are not isolated records. They are interconnected. When you remember something, you remember a whole bunch of details and context.

When you see a certain face, you know that person’s name. You remember the first time you met the person. You remember where the person works. You remember where the person lives. You remember whether you have a good relationship with the person.

# The paper’s central argument

The paper’s central argument is that the hippocampus uses key-value pairs to retrieve memories.

When you see a face, that’s the key. The memory of that person is the value. When you remember the person’s name, that’s the key. The memory of that person is the value.

Think of the hippocampus as a highway system. Suppose I see Axel’s face, that’s the key. The hippocampus is like a highway system. The memory of Axel is the value.

The paper uses a lot of fancy words and ideas from machine learning. But fundamentally, the argument is simple and plausible.

# The paper’s flaws

The paper is flawed because the experiment is faulty.

The experiment is a gamble that half of the participants will be right and half of the participants will be wrong.

The participants watch a sequence of images. They are asked to judge whether a certain image is the “right image”. The experiment is designed so that half of the participants are right and half of the participants are wrong.

The experiment is designed so that the participants are not able to tell which image is the “right image”. The participants don’t know which image is the “right image” from the sequence. They are forced to make a guess.

The experiment is a gamble. But the experiment is not a fair gamble. The experiment is rigged. The experiment is rigged in favor of the hypothesis.

It’s in favor of the hypothesis because the experiment is designed to be a gamble. The experiment is designed so that half of the participants will be right and half of the participants will be wrong.

Suppose the experiment is repeated many times. What will happen if I were to make a wild guess? Suppose I said that every time a participant got a question wrong, the participant would see a hallucination.

The experiment would show that every time a participant got a question wrong, the participant saw a hallucination, but not when they got a question right.

The experiment would show that the hippocampus is responsible for hallucinations.

That’s not a fair experiment.

A better experiment would be to ask the participants to judge whether a certain image is the “right image” and then ask them to explain why they think that image is the “right image”. Then, the experiment would show that the hippocampus is responsible for memory retrieval.

# Why I remain convinced

I remain convinced because the paper’s central argument is simple and plausible.

The paper’s central argument is that the hippocampus uses key-value pairs to retrieve memories. That’s simple and plausible. It’s simple and plausible because memories are not isolated records. They are interconnected. When you remember something, you remember a whole bunch of details and context.

I think a better design of the experiment would be to simply record the activity of the hippocampus while the participants are watching a sequence of images. Then, the experiment would show that the hippocampus is responsible for memory retrieval.

I suspect that the hippocampus is doing the memory retrieval for all sorts of memories. When we see a face, when we hear a name, when we see a place, when we see a book, when we see a movie, when we see a song, etc. I’m convinced that “key-value pairs” are a good way to think about how the hippocampus works.
