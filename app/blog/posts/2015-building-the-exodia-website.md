Imagine you’re at Exodia, the annual cultural fest of IIT Mandi. You heard about a concert event from a friend and want to check out when it is happening. You type exod.ia/14 into your browser.

You’re a few minutes late — the site has been recently updated to exod.ia/15. You are automatically redirected to the new page as you click enter.

Now you have to figure out where the music events are listed on the page. It isn’t immediately apparent. After a few seconds of looking around, you see that music events are categorized under the vertical “Cultural Events”. You click on “More”.

There are so many events! You squint and try to find the one you’re looking for. You are forced to reminisce about how easy things were back in 2014 when there was a dropdown list of events categorized by day for both days of the fest.

![Screen Shot 2015–10–25 at 11.41.33 AM.png](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*GQA0XQatQL8K_rObycB3wQ.png)

You get to the event page. There are a couple of sentences about the event and the timing. The details are pretty accurate because the team had to constantly update it. You quickly change your mind to check out a different event, chitchat with a friend who’s also on the website, and then get back to the schedule.

You’re frustrated and disappointed. You’d rather talk to people than figure out where events are listed on the website.

I absolutely hated the experience. I’m glad I didn’t keep the 2-day event schedule because the schedule kept on changing. I’m also glad I implemented a smooth scrolling setup because it gave a modern feel to the website. I wish I had an idea of which events were most sought after so I could have used that for analytics and also to help me design a better website.

![Screen Shot 2015–10–22 at 12.27.57 AM.png](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*b23H8l_G36zqXW6lL6zR3g.png)

But as much as I hated every moment of maintaining the website, I learned a lot and reignited my passion for web design again. Stepping back a little, this was the first time I was building a website for a huge event in such a short amount of time with so many people working on the different parts of the website. I was the only one who was working on the entire website. The timeline was tight for me. I only had 2.5 weeks to design and build a website from scratch.

My original plan was to build the website on Wordpress.com. I started setting up templates and themes only to realize that Wordpress had limitations in terms of design. I wanted to experiment with JavaScript and CSS. I didn’t want to use something that was restrictive. I wanted to learn to build something from scratch. I decided to take up the challenge and hand-code the website without the use of any framework.

Throughout the 2.5 weeks, I ran around the hostel or stayed awake throughout the night to build the website. It was tiring and frustrating but it was definitely worth it. If there’s one screen in particular that I like the most, it’s this one:

![Screen Shot 2015–10–22 at 12.29.21 AM.png](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*qI3CqH8u_5NNI-7l3_d8xQ.png)

I’m proud of it. If I had to redo the entire website, I’d definitely keep this one. It’s simple yet catchy. The skyline is a remix of the Hyderabad skyline. The artwork on the sides was done by the Exodia design team. I would definitely change the font type on the new website.
