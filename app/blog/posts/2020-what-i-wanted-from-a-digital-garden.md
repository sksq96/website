If you are reading this, you probably are interested in the Digital Gardening ethos, and probably have seen a bunch of blogs, websites, and even twitter threads about it. Now, why this one? Why do we need yet another blog about it? What do I bring to the table? What I think is the underlying feeling behind people's enthusiasm for Digital Gardening?

For me, it was a search for a feeling of liveliness in my static Jekyll based personal website. I had been interested in exploring IndieWeb stuff, and was planning on adding a bunch of things to my Jekyll based site. Then I came across this concept of digital gardening, and I was intrigued. I liked the idea, and I wanted to make my website feel like a garden and less like a static page.

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dhxq7roas/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto/v1591257023/Jekyll%20vs%20Garden_lqrqk5.png)

The way I wanted to make my website feel more lively was through the use of links and backlinks. I was reading about and experimenting with Zettelkasten and Bi-directional linking, and this was a natural way to make my blog/notes link to themselves, and to other blogs/notes. 

For example, I wanted to be able to link to an interesting paper I was reading, and share it with the world. The way I would do that would be by just posting a note in my garden, `[[Paper Review: Paper Title]]`. Once I have posted it, I can link to it from anywhere in the garden. If I was writing a blog on NLP, I could do this: `... For example, we can apply SiteNet in natural language processing, like in [[Paper Review: Paper Title]] which talks about ....`. 

Now, if someone is reading this blog, they will see a link to the paper review, and if they are reading the paper review, they will see a link to the blog. Since I am using the Obsidian style of linking, `[[ ]]`, it also makes it very easy to search for the paper review, and see where all I have linked to the blog and the paper review.

So, I set out to explore this idea, and created a [Digital Garden](https://shubham.live) using [Maggie Appleton's Digital Garden Starter Template](https://github.com/maggieappleton/digital-garden). It was really easy to set up, and I was able to get my site up and running in a couple of hours. 

It is a great way to share your notes and thoughts with the world, and I'm excited to see how it works out for me. I'll write a separate blog post on the way I set up my digital garden.

A question that I have been thinking about is this:

How do I stay engaged with my notes and blog once the initial excitement of setting up the digital garden has worn off? How do I ensure that I keep my digital garden lively?

I think the answer is in a combination of things. I need to make sure that I am using my digital garden to share my thoughts and ideas with the world, and that I am using it to learn from others. I also need to make sure that I am keeping my digital garden up to date, and that I am adding new content to it regularly.
