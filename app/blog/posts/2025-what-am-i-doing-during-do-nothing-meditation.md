I recently came back to Shinzen Young’s “do nothing” meditation (listen on Youtube).

Towards the end of the session, he says something along the lines of:

> when you become aware that you’re trying to manage or control your attention in any way, just drop it

That seems to imply that there is an action one takes (dropping the trying to control your attention), which is something that I noticed.

But then he says something like:

> when you become aware that you are dropping the trying to control your attention, just let that go too

Huh? So I let go of dropping the trying to control my attention? And now I’m doing… what exactly? This is supposed to be a “do nothing” meditation.

But, I suppose, when I become aware that I’m letting go of dropping the trying to control my attention, I just let that go too. And then if I become aware that I’m letting go of letting go of dropping the trying to control my attention, I let that go too.

This is getting kind of confusing, and makes me wonder if I was doing it wrong. Maybe there’s some kind of action I can take that isn’t an action. If I could just take that action, then I would be doing nothing.

I was listening to [Meditation on Emptiness talks](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLgcJPeW3e-mSKgAaJDjmdgNmO-8J9K0yq) by Rob Burbea during a 2 week meditation retreat in February, and I’m reminded of a passage that stood out to me (I’m not sure if you can understand it without having watched the talk, but I’ll include it in case you want to try):

> As long as we're taking the apparent arising of the appearance of this or that, as an indication that there's something there, that it's a thing, that there's an entity, that there's a self, that there's an other, that there's a world, that there's something... that's the subtle ignorance. It's not the seeing or the hearing, or the tasting or the touching. It's not even the arising of the sense of something being there, or a thing being there. It's the taking of that. It's the grasping at that. It's the believing in that. It's the not seeing the empty nature of that. That's the subtle ignorance. That's the subtle delusion.

I think this is the exact wording that helped it really click for me, it was like a major aha moment, but it’s hard to explain.
