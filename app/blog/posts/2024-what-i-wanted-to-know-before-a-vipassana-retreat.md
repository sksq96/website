_I wrote this blog before attending a retreat. It is an account of what I was wondering at the time._

I'm going to a 10-day silent Vipassana meditation retreat in 2 weeks. I've never done this before. The idea of doing a retreat was brought up by a friend of mine. I was and am still hesitating in committing to it.

My hesitation is about whether I'm ready to take this step. Meditation is something that I've wanted to do for a long time. I've actually tried to start many times in the past. But it never really stuck. And I'm not sure why. I've tried all sorts of apps. I've tried different meditation styles. I've gone to a meditation class. I've done group meditation with friends. Yet, somehow I wasn't able to pick up the habit. I think I should be able to do it now. I think my life is quite stable and I have the time and space to dedicate to something like this. But I don't have a foundation of meditation as a habit in my daily life. So I'm not sure if a 10-day silent retreat is a good idea.

The retreats are held according to the tradition of S.N. Goenka. I've been reading a bit about it. I'm still not sure what "Vipassana" really means. So I'm looking for a description.

What does this strange word mean? And why would anyone want to do this? I find the Vipassana Wikipedia page difficult to understand. Here's a better description of the word:

Vipassana, literally seeing things as they are, implies no beliefs. A direct experience of reality. Non judgmental awareness.

The goal of the meditation is to arrive at a state of non-judgmental awareness. And this is achieved through body scanning. Body scanning has been something that I've been wanting to try but never did. So this is exciting.

There can be different types of retreats for different lengths of time. Goenka retreats are always 10 days. And there can be different types of Vipassana meditation. Goenka retreats are about body scanning and "seeing things as they are".

I wonder what the daily schedule is like. There's obviously a lot of meditation. But I'm sure there's a lot of time for rest too. I'm guessing that I will have to do a lot of physical chores to support the retreat. And I'm assuming there's a lot of time for meals. Maybe they have extra long meals to accommodate for the fact that everyone is silent?

Here's the schedule:

There's a lot of meditation. It's 10 hours of meditation everyday. That's a lot. I've never meditated for more than 30 minutes at a time. But I've heard that it's possible for people to meditate for hours at a time. I wonder how that feels like.

There's a lot of talking. I wonder what the talking is about. I'm guessing that it's mostly about the meditation. That's a lot of talking! I wonder if people talk about other things. I wonder if I will be able to understand everything.

I also wonder if I'll be able to sleep at 9:30pm. I'm not used to going to bed that early. I'm guessing that I'll be quite tired after the day. So maybe I'll be able to sleep early.

I wonder if the schedule is flexible. I wonder if I can skip some of the meditation sessions. I wonder if I can take a nap during the day. I wonder if I can do some exercise. I wonder if I can read or write. I'm sure there's a lot of rules and restrictions. But I wonder if there's some flexibility.

The entirety of the retreat is in silence. The only time I will be allowed to speak is during the discussion sessions. And maybe during the meals. And I won't be allowed to look at other people. I wonder how that will feel like. I wonder if I will be able to go without looking at my phone. And I wonder if I will be able to go without looking at other people. And I wonder if I will be able to go without looking at myself.

There are different kinds of teachers. There are teachers who teach the meditation. There are teachers who teach the philosophy. There are teachers who teach the schedule. There are teachers who teach the rules.

There's usually 3-5 assistant teachers. They are there to help with the meditation. They are there to answer questions. They are there to help with the schedule. They are there to help with the rules.

The main teacher is the one who has been trained by Goenka. They are the ones who teach the meditation. They are the ones who teach the philosophy. They are the ones who teach the schedule. They are the ones who teach the rules.

I wonder if I will meet the teachers. I wonder if I will be able to ask them questions. I wonder if I will be able to talk to them. I wonder if I will be able to meditate with them.

The retreat is free. You don't have to pay anything. But you are expected to donate at the end of the retreat. I wonder how much I should donate. I wonder if I will be able to donate. I wonder if I will be able to afford to donate.

There's a question of whether I should go or not. I'm still not sure. I've been hesitating for a while. I'm not sure if I'm ready. I'm not sure if I'm prepared. I'm not sure if I'm able to do it.

A few weeks ago, I began the process of signing up. I went through the questionnaire. I read the FAQs. I read the code of conduct. I read the schedule. I read the donation page. I read the testimonials. I read the reviews.

After all that, I decided to not sign up. I've been hesitating since then. I'm not sure what to do.
