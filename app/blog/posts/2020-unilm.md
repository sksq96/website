## The BERT Model and Fine-tuning on SQuAD 1.1

I'm not going to go on an in-depth review of what the BERT model is. That's not the point of this article. Instead, I will give you my high-level understanding of the BERT model.

I like to think of the BERT model as a word prediction model, in a similar way to how autocomplete works on your phone. It tries to understand how you write and predict the most likely word to come next. Let's dig a bit deeper into that.

### What is BERT?

BERT stands for Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers. So, it's a language model that is based on transformers. Transformers are a type of recurrent neural network. There is a reason for this, and I won't go into it. The core idea behind this model is that it should be able to understand the context of a word by looking at the words that come before and after it. This is different from the traditional language models that only look at the words that come before it.

The "Bidirectional" part of the name is what makes this model unique. It's able to look at the words that come before and after a word to understand the context of the word.

### What is Fine-tuning?

Fine-tuning is the process of taking a pre-trained model and training it on a specific task. For example, the BERT model was pre-trained on a large corpus of text. This means that it has a general understanding of language. However, it doesn't have a specific understanding of the SQuAD 1.1 task. So, we need to fine-tune it on the SQuAD 1.1 dataset to make it understand the task.

### What is SQuAD 1.1?

SQuAD 1.1 is a dataset that contains questions and answers. The questions are generated from a Wikipedia article and the answers are generated by a human. The goal of the task is to predict the answer to a question given the question and the Wikipedia article.
