I'll be brief. I'm not a doctor and I don't know your lifestyle. I'm not saying you should microdose. I'm not saying you shouldn't microdose. All I'm going to say is that you should figure out for yourself whether microdosing produces any measurable differences for you. Maybe it does, maybe it doesn't. Also maybe you'll "feel" a difference, but that feeling will be due to a placebo effect.

I'm going to link you to a few resources soon, but first: why did I take this seriously? I saw it in the May 2020 reading list of Dan Carlin's Hardcore History Podcast. The reading list links to a 2017 Vox article, in which the author went on a quest to figure out the effects of microdosing LSD. Not only did he microdose LSD, he also microdosed mushrooms, MDMA, MDA, and even cannabis.

What did he find? He was able to tell if he had taken cannabis or not, but it was very hard to distinguish between the other drugs and a placebo.

This article linked to a 2019 study in which a group of people was given microdoses of LSD, codeine, or a placebo. The double-blind study (meaning neither the participants nor the researchers knew what drug was given) found that the LSD and placebo were indistinguishable.

This then brings me to the most recent study I want to link to: a microdosing self-blinding study published in eLife on April 14, 2021. 

Here, the participants were asked to prepare their own microdoses and then encapsulate them. For a 4 week period, they took the capsules without knowing whether they were placebos or not. The results were interesting, and I assume you'll have to read the study to get all the details. But here are some interesting points:

- The researchers found that the microdoses were effective.
- The participants' pre-planned microdosing days were associated with better mood, increased focus, and self-efficacy. (So when they expected to be taking a microdose, they felt better.)
- But when they actually took a microdose, it wasn't associated with any of these things.
- One of the main things the researchers found was that the participants reported positive results if they guessed they had taken a microdose, regardless of whether they actually took a microdose or not.

These are just highlights from the results, but I think that's enough for me to stop and say: "I would like to run my own self-blinding study."

I don't currently microdose. (Update: I have microdosed in the past, but in an uncontrolled setting.) But maybe the results of this study would be different for me. Here's my plan:

- I'm going to microdose LSD.
- I'm going to keep a journal (in a Google Doc) where every day I write about my day and how it went.
- For the first 4 weeks, I'm going to use one of the self-blinded microdosing apps to determine when I'm getting a microdose and a placebo.
- For the second 4 weeks, I'm going to keep microdosing, but every day I'll write down whether I think I've taken a microdose or a placebo.
- If my ability to predict whether I'm taking a microdose is better than chance, I'll continue microdosing (with the help of one of the self-blinded microdosing apps).
- If my ability to predict is no better than chance, I'll either stop microdosing or microdose every day.

I've been working on a script that will help me predict whether I'm taking a microdose by analyzing my journal entries. More on that in a future post. For now, I just want to briefly talk about how I'm planning on microdosing.

I'm using the Dose app for Android. I found the Selfblinding app for iPhone, but I still haven't found a way of microdosing using that app with LSD. I'm thinking I'll just use the Dose app to collect the data, then do my own analysis.

The Dose app wants me to take a microdose every 3 days, but the eLife study points out that 3 days is too long between doses. I'm going to go with 2 days.

The Dose app asks for 3 different microdoses. At first I was just diluting LSD in distilled water and putting the water in capsules. But thanks to the comment section on Hacker News, I found out that this might not be a good idea. In particular, the LSD (which is a solid) might react with the capsule and become less potent. So now I'm dissolving the LSD in vodka. I'll let the vodka evaporate and then put the leftover solids in the capsules.

I'm going to buy a bag of size 00 capsules. Then I'm going to use a syringe to put 2-15 microliters (uL) of vodka into each capsule. Only after I've done that will I add the LSD. That way I can dilute it to varying amounts.

But here's my question: how many ug of LSD do I want per capsule? I don't know. The literature says anywhere between 5-50 ug. I'm assuming 22 ug is a decent starting place. I'll keep track of how much I'm diluting the LSD in vodka, and how many uL of vodka I'm adding to the capsules. That way I should be able to figure out how many ug I'm adding to each.

I'll write another post when I start actually taking the microdoses and when I'm done with the study. Until then, take care!
