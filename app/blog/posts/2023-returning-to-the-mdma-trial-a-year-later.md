I haven’t mentioned the MDMA trial for PTSD since I covered it in May, 2021. And I had a specific reason for that.

In this article, I’ll discuss what I had been looking for when I returned to it in May 2023. And I’ll also cover what the confirmatory trial found.

In May 2023, I was browsing through my tabs from May 2022. I saw the tab I had opened for the study and decided to open it once again. I also had some doubts that I wanted to clear up.

I wanted to know how the researchers treated the missing data. And I also wanted to check the trial protocol and what the researchers had reported.

If you check the limitations of the confirmatory study, the authors note:

> These results should be considered in the context of several limitations. The wide confidence intervals around the change and differences in CAPS-5 scores between the first phase 3 study and this study may be due to variations in the populations between the studies; however, both studies have wide confidence intervals, which could be due to the small sample sizes. Missing data were imputed using the mixed model for repeated measures’ imputation method, which provides a conservative estimate of the treatment effect. The study was not designed to assess the increased risk for suicidal ideation or behavior. Only 8% of potential participants screened for the study were deemed ineligible due to meeting suicide risk exclusion criteria, because most participants were already enrolled in a phase 2 open-label study or a phase 3 open-label study when the suicide risk exclusion criteria were added to the protocol. Since this study is the first phase 3 study of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, the generalizability of the results to other populations is not yet clear. Larger studies are needed to determine the safety and efficacy of MDMA-assisted therapy in diverse populations. With respect to functional unblinding, 46.3% of participants in the MDMA group versus 21% of participants in the placebo group reported that they did not know or were unsure about which treatment they had received. Previous studies have also shown that blinding of participants in MDMA studies may not be feasible. In a 2008 study of the safety and efficacy of MDMA for subjects with PTSD, 68% of subjects in the MDMA group and 11% in the placebo group correctly guessed their treatment assignment. With respect to the functional unblinding of therapists, independent raters were used to assess the primary outcome measure of CAPS-5 to minimize the potential for bias. The amount of support provided by these therapists may exceed that available in all clinical settings.

My first concern was with the small sample size. And with it, a related question: How did they address the missing data?

In the Methods section of the confirmatory trial, the authors noted:

> The analysis of change in CAPS-5 score (primary outcome) was conducted using a mixed model for repeated measures, which included data collected from all randomized participants.

In this, they followed the same method used in the phase 3 trial.

The study protocols of both the phase 3 trial and the confirmatory study are available on ClinicalTrials.gov.

The phase 3 trial protocol can be found here, by clicking on the ‘Study Documents’ button that appears on the right side of the page. The confirmatory trial’s protocol is on this page.

Both the study designs are very similar. The difference that I cared about is the sample size. The phase 3 study ran a small sample of 90 participants. The confirmatory study ran a sample of only 104 participants.

In both studies, the authors used the mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) method to handle missing data.

MDMA + therapy showed an effect size of 0.7 (95% CI 0.2, 1.2) compared to placebo + therapy in the confirmatory trial.

In the phase 3 trial, the effect size was slightly higher: 0.91 (95% CI 0.58, 1.25).

The slight difference could be attributed to the small sample size. And yet, it is hard to ignore that the authors of the confirmatory trial did not highlight the effect size.

They focused on the change in CAPS-5 scores.

Interestingly, the confirmatory trial was published in September 2023, a few months after I browsed through my tabs. So, it wasn’t there for me to find in May 2023.
