“Be kinder to myself” is advice that’s often thrown around. And while I appreciate the general sentiment, it often feels like it’s talking about self-care, and what I need help with is more specific.

For example, if I’m in a situation where I’m working on something challenging, then working on something challenging is precisely what I want to be doing. But I get frustrated and discouraged when things don’t go well. “Be kinder to myself” is not a helpful reminder, as what I need is a way to deal with the challenge in front of me.

If I were to translate “be kinder to myself” to something actionable in that context, it’s probably something like: **“Accept that you don’t know the answers to the problem that you’re facing”**.

I think it’s a bit of a stretch to say that this is what someone means by “be kinder to yourself”, but I can see the connection. If I’m facing a challenge that I don’t know how to solve, then I need to accept that you don’t know the answers, and if I’m beating myself up for not knowing the answers (“I’m so stupid I don’t know how to do this”), then that’s not being kind to myself.

Anyway, I’m glad I’ve worked out how to translate “be kinder to yourself” to something actionable for me, since it’s a bit of a cliché by now.

## What are you still ashamed of?

> What are you still ashamed of?
>
> If you take a moment to pay attention to it you can probably feel it. Maybe it’s a memory that still makes you squirm. Maybe it’s a feeling that you’re not good enough. Maybe it’s the way you’ve treated someone. Maybe it’s a part of yourself that you’ve been running from.
>
> Maybe you’re ashamed of being ashamed.
>
> What if you could go to that place of shame and offer it some understanding, some acceptance, some compassion?

I read this passage in Pete Kahn’s book The Path to Freedom, and it’s stuck with me.

I’m not going to write about this in depth here, but I do want to share that I’m doing a mindfulness practice where each day I go to the place of one of my shames for a minute or two and offer it some acceptance, understanding, and compassion (as Pete recommends). I decided to do this because I find that my shames affect me in big ways, and they’re worth taking seriously.

I’ve done that for 8 days now, and it’s been quite an experience. The biggest change for me has been that I now tend not to avoid the feeling of shame. When it comes up, I’m much more able to notice it and explore it. It’s still a bit unsettling, but I seem to be moving towards a place of “oh, shame is here, I can be with you”.

## Using Flux

I’ve been using the Flux browser on my phone for a few days now. The main thing I like about it is that it has a much more compact view. I’m viewing Flux on my phone, and it shows a list of links (sometimes with previews) that you can scan.

I’ve been using it a bit like a read-it-later service (i.e. I’m using it as a place to save links that I want to read later), but I’m considering using it as my only browser on my phone. The main reason to do that is that Flux makes it very easy to get a sense of what things I have unread, which is useful.

If you’re interested in this experiment, you can find the Flux app in the iOS App Store.

I don’t have a good sense of what astrologically attuned people do during a new moon. I know that the full moon is a time of releasing and the new moon is a time of new beginnings and starting things. But I don’t know if those are good enough reasons to start something.

I do think it’s kinda cool to make your life a bit more attuned to the cycles of nature. So even if there’s no reason to do anything special on the full moon or the new moon, I think doing something special is a cool thing to do anyway.

If you want to do anything special today, I’d say start with “What kind of special would I like to do today?” and let your instincts guide you.
