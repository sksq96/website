Inspired by this post by Paul G, I built something similar with my Macbook Pro. You can try it out here.

I’ve been learning more about Hindustani classical music. One unique instrument they play is the harmonium. It’s a keyboard instrument where you push air through the pipes by operating a bellow.

I wanted to have a harmonium and play along with the Indian music I listen to. I tried to find an app on my phone, but couldn’t find anything I liked. Either the instrument didn’t sound like a harmonium, or it was too difficult to maintain a constant drone.

Today I found out that MacBook Pros come with Apple Silicon, and that comes with a bunch of sensors. This includes the angle of the laptop lid.

So I decided to build a harmonium . . . on my MacBook Pro.

How it works

The MacBook lid is used to make the sound loud and soft, just like the bellow on a harmonium. Since there’s no pressure sensor, I use the speed and angle of the lid to change the volume.

Open the lid more, and the instrument sound gets louder. Close the lid and the sound gets softer.

The keys on the keyboard play notes.

Watch my demo here.

The video has a lot of background noise. I was in a coffee shop.

What’s next?

I’m still thinking through how to make the instrument feel more like an actual harmonium. Another thing that would be cool is to record the sound and let people upload it to social media.

If you want to play around with it yourself, clone my repo and try it out.

The code is a little cursed because I was debugging with Cursor. I’ve been trying to use it as a substitute for Google as I learn how to make music.
