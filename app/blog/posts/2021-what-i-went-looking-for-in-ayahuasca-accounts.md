There was a point in my life where I was probably reading every ayahuasca account I could find. I think the internet was a better place when it had a rich ecosystem of lawyer and psychedelic blogs.

I was trying to answer this question: How do you know if a psychedelic retreat is going to be good?
A lot of people have written about what makes a good psychonautic trip. But how do you know if the place you are staying will be good to you?

I read one account by a writer in the New Yorker who went to a retreat in Peru after the death of a friend. The retreat she went to was very stylish. The food was good. The building was extremely fancy. 

You should read the whole thing. I think it's good. I also think it's a little bit shitty to go on an ayahuasca retreat and make fun of the people who work there.

I read a second account by a writer in the Drift who went to a retreat in Jamaica. The retreat was *not* stylish. The food was fine, the bathroom was icky, and the bedrooms smelled like mildew.

The writer, again, is quite funny. He makes fun of his pretentious fellow travelers. He also writes really well about the experience.

The second writer had a great time. The first writer didn't have such a great time. I'm not sure if that's because of the setting, or because of their personal expectations beforehand, and the fact that one had recently been traumatized by the death of a friend, and the other had come for a more cheerful kind of fun.

There are a ton of stories out there about different kinds of ayahuasca retreats. I think there's a lot you can learn from reading them, but I don't know if you can *really* tell how a space will feel before going there. The most upsetting retreat that I've ever read about was a really nice one. The most welcoming space that I've ever been in was cleaner than a motel but far from luxurious.
