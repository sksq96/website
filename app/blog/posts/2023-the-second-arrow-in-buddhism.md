Reflection on a book I read in June 2022: You Are Here, Discovering the Magic of the Present Moment, Thich Nhat Hanh, 2010

This book, which is a thin hardcover, contains a number of talks and writings by Thich Nhat Hanh. The book focuses on mindfulness and how to live in the present moment. I read this book in June 2022, and it inspired me to start a meditation practice.

One passage from this book that I highlighted is the following:

One important teaching of the Buddha is the teaching about the two arrows. The Buddha said that when we suffer, it is as if two arrows have penetrated us. The first arrow represents the contact between our sense organs and the objects of the senses and the feelings that arise from that contact. 
For example, when our body comes into contact with a stone, we feel physical pain. This is the first arrow. But after we've been hit by the first arrow, we continue to be hit by a second arrow, which is our mental suffering. We have a feeling of injustice, a feeling of anger, irritation, fear, and despair. The second arrow represents all these afflictions, which bring additional pain and sorrow.

The second arrow is something we add to our suffering when we start thinking about how unfair it is that we stubbed our toe, and how that is not the first time we've stubbed our toe, and how stubbing our toe will ruin the rest of our day, and how we hate that we stubbed our toe, and how we wish we hadn't stubbed our toe, and...

The Buddha said that all of us are prone to being hit by the second arrow. We cannot prevent the first arrow from penetrating us, but the Buddha said that if we practice mindfulness we can prevent the second arrow from penetrating us.

I went back to this quote a few months after I read it, when I was struggling with some heavy feelings. I was really feeling the pain of the second arrow, and I thought it might be helpful to reflect on the first arrow. I was feeling discouraged and frustrated with myself, and I was getting worked up about my feelings. I was so surprised when I realized that my suffering came from the second arrow. I was not in any kind of physical pain, but I was adding a lot of mental pain. I sat down and reflected on the one unchanging fact that the second arrow was hinged on: I was feeling sad. I was able to accept that sadness as the first arrow, and as something that was a part of my experience, rather than something that was wrong with me. That helped me to let go of some of the mental pain I was adding to my experience.

What I love about this analogy is the way it separates the physical pain from the mental pain. However, the analogy doesn't always fit so well. In this passage from the Buddha, the two arrows are sequential, one after the other. But that is not always how it works. Sometimes there is no first arrow, and there is just the second arrow. Sometimes there is no second arrow, and there is just the first arrow. Sometimes the two arrows are simultaneous, and it is hard to tell them apart. The physical pain and the mental pain can be so intertwined that it is hard to separate them. But I think the analogy is still helpful, even if it is not perfect.
