I was trying to understand the human brain, because I saw that as a path to understanding human emotions, and to figuring out how to be happier. I first watched a video about the work of Hubel and Wiesel, in which they recorded the firing of a neuron in the visual cortex while a cat sat and stared at a wall with a visual stimulus. As a dark bar on the wall moved around, the neuron remained silent. But when the bar passed through a certain angle, the neuron fired frantically. I found a recording of their experiment, and I played with the stimulus to find the position at which the neuron would fire.

![](images/hubel-wiesel-stimulus.gif)

I imagined trying to determine the function of neurons in the brain by listening to their firing as I fed the creature various stimuli. For example, if I didn't know that a neuron was sensitive to the angle of a bar, how could I discover it? I came up with an experiment that involved showing the neuron a line moving around in a circle against a white background. The neuron fires only when the black bar passes through a certain angle.

![](images/angle-neuron-stimulus.gif)

But if the experiment used a white bar against a dark background, the neuron would not fire. So the neuron is not sensitive to a particular angle, but rather to a particular angle of a dark bar against a white background.

![](images/white-bar-stimulus.gif)

This could be tested by showing the neuron a circle with a dark half and a white half. The neuron fires when the circle rotates to a certain angle.

![](images/half-black-circle-stimulus.gif)

But if the neuron were interested in the angle of a dark bar, it would also fire when the circle rotated to the opposite side, because when it is on the opposite side, it is still a dark bar at the same angle. However, the neuron does not fire when the circle is on the opposite side. So the neuron is not sensitive to the angle of a dark bar, but rather to the angle of a light-to-dark edge.
