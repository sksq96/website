I’ve been trying to figure out what the organ piece in the Interstellar docking scene is. Originally, I thought it was from a Bach toccata or fuge, but I couldn’t find it.

Thankfully, I found this video by Roger Sayer. The piece is actually an original composition by Hans Zimmer. It was very fun to see how the piece is made and how the organs are played.

The scene is genius. The pulse of the organ continuously matches or compliments the rotation of the space station. Each note crescendos as it points to the characters, and then it fades out as it revolves away. The harmony matches the state of the scene and how the characters are feeling.

To me, it feels like the music is always advancing and the characters and station are always advancing, although there are moments of silence and stillness. When the wormhole is revealed for the first time, the harmonies make me feel wonder and excitement.

I wish I could feel the sheer terror that is shown by the characters (entire worlds and galaxies being revealed). I wish I could have that sort of emotional reaction to the film instead of feeling like it was too over-the-top.

The organ piece is comprised of three different themes with overlapping sections. The main theme is the original “ticking clock” organ piece.

The second theme is a variation of the first. Zimmer replaced the organ with a piano and used a different harmonic progression. For me, this is where the music really gets intense, and it matches the sheer terror that the characters are feeling.

The third theme is a variation of the second. It’s the piano piece, backed by a large orchestra. For me, this is the most intense section of the track, and it definitely matches the gravity of the scene.
