I’m going to dedicate this post to a journal, my own.

Let’s start with a hypothetical. You have to decide how you’re going to journal. You must choose one of these options, and you cannot change your mind later. What do you choose?

**Option 1:**

```
- [ ] Finish reading a book
- [ ] Meet friends
```

**Option 2:**

```
I was looking at the book when I realized I was hungry. I felt like I needed to eat something to be able to focus.
So I got out of the living room and went to the kitchen. 
I opened the fridge to find a Blueberry pie
```

**Option 3:**

```
Did I make the right decision?
```

Let’s imagine you choose option 1. You’re productive; you’re ready to get things done. You write a list of things you want to do in your journal. You’re a diligent person. You write that you want to finish the book you’re reading, you want to meet your friends, maybe you even put a timeline to your tasks. At the end of the day, you check them out, for a nice dopamine hit. You go to sleep.

You wake up in the morning. Do you remember what you did yesterday? At some level, you know you’re reading a book and you’ve caught up with your friends. You’re aware of the list, you know where it is, you know you can go back and look at it. You’ve lost track of the time you had a coffee with your friend John.

Figment reads your journal. It can answer what your goals are. It knows you can read a book and get out of the house. It can tell you that you’re reading a book. It has some idea of what sort of books.

Let’s imagine you choose option 2. Every day, you write a scene from your day. You write about what you do and how you feel. You write about the coffee you have with your friend John. You write about your book. You write about your feelings. It’s emotional, it’s detail-oriented, it’s immersive.

You wake up in the morning. Do you remember what you did yesterday? Figment does. It can tell you it was a hot day. It knows you had a coffee with your friend John. It knows you were reading a book. It knows you were feeling stressed. It knows you were feeling happy. It knows you were feeling sad.

Let’s imagine you choose option 3. You write a single line every day. You start the day by asking yourself a question. You can choose a new question, or you can choose an old question. You might even choose a question you don’t know the answer to. You go through the day with this question in mind. You might even ask yourself this question again at the end of the day. You might even ask yourself this question again the next day.

You wake up in the morning. Do you remember what you did yesterday? Figment does. It can tell you what your decision was. It knows you were thinking about the decision. It knows you were thinking about your life. It knows you were thinking about your book. It knows you were thinking about your friend John. It knows whether you’re comfortable making decisions.

The thing about journaling is that the more you write, the more you’re able to remember.

But the more you write, the more you’re able to forget. Do you want to remember the coffee dates you had in college? Or do you want to remember the time you went to the beach with your friends?

It depends on what you’re looking to get out of your journal.

You have to decide what you want to remember.

I’d like to share a snippet from my journal:

```
I've been feeling tired lately. I'm not sure why. I'm not sure what I'm doing wrong.
```

I’m not sure why I chose to write this. I’m not sure what I was thinking. I’m not sure what I was feeling. I’m not sure what I was doing. But I do know I was feeling tired. And I do know I was feeling confused. And I do know that this is why I chose to write this.
