If you have followed my blog over the past month you might have noticed that I have mentioned psychedelics a few times. Quite unrelated to this, I also wrote a post about bread and its ingredients. I have been reading Albert Camus' Myth of Sisyphus and I felt like I should've written about it in the same style as the other posts. So here it goes.

First, a little bit of context.

I have had a hard time trying to understand Camus. I have listened to a few lectures, read a few of his essays, and I am still not sure I have grasped his main ideas. So I won't be surprised if I am criticized for the content of this post. I am not a philosophy major, and I have no pretension for being one. I am simply exploring a new domain of knowledge for me, and I am writing about it here because I think that it is an essential part of my work.

Camus' main question is formulated in many ways. But I will rephrase it in the simplest way, that I can bear to think about. His question is:

Why are you still alive?

I read the book for the first time in early 2020. It took a lot of time to read. It was hard for me to grasp concepts, because I didn't have a deep philosophy background. It took me a few months to read (I didn't read every day).

I found myself thinking a lot to Camus' question. As it was my first time being confronted to it that way, I found it hard to find an answer.

I remember that I was thinking about getting a cat. I was living alone in a big city. I had a good job, but I wasn't content with my life. I was thinking about a cat because I felt like something was missing. Now, I don't know how to make you feel the same way that I felt. But I felt a profound emptiness. I didn't know why I was living. I was at a crossroads in my life. Two options were available to me: go back to school to pursue a PhD, or take action and start building my own business. I was skeptical about both options, because of my past experiences.

I was torn. Until I finally made a decision.

Why am I still alive? I don't know. But I know that I have to make a decision.

I decided to make a decision.

I decided not to do a PhD. I decided to go on my own, and see if I could make a living by doing my own business. I am not sure where I am going. But I feel like I am finally moving.

I read the book again earlier this month. My life is not perfect. But I am content with my decision. I am making it work. I am trying new things, and I am learning by doing so. I am taking action. I am not trying to escape my reality. I am trying to understand it, and make the best of it.

I am no psychologist, but I read a lot about the topic. I think that the feelings that I had when I was reading Camus' book were similar to those of an existential crisis. I don't know if this is true in general for people who end up reading Camus. What I know is that I am glad that I did.

Camus gives an answer to his question. He says that we have to embrace the absurdity of life. We have to embrace the fact that life is not perfect and we have to find happiness in it.

I think that I have found it in my own way. I am not sure if I will ever be able to build something on my own. But I am happy with the decision that I have made. I am happy to have read Camus. I am happy to be alive.

I know that some people might think that I am writing a romantic and childish post. But I think that I have found a solution to my existential crisis.

I read Camus, I made a decision because of it, and I am content with my life right now.

If you have read Camus, I would love to hear your thoughts on it. How did you feel when you first read it? Do you agree with his ideas?
