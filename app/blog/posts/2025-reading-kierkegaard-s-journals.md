It is easy to find Kierkegaard’s writing. The Wikipedia page on him has a link to his journal entries. They are less easy to decipher, but we can start with the entries from January of each year.

The first entry for January, in 1834, when he was 20 years old, doesn’t have much interesting to say. It’s about his cousin, who had a scar and was called “the beautiful cousin,” and about his uncle, who had become a widower.

The first entry for January 1835 is more interesting. Kierkegaard writes of a time when he was in a new mood every night. He is 21. 

“What I really need is to be clear about what I am to do, not what I am to know, except in the sense that a kind of understanding must precede every action. It is a question of understanding myself, of seeing what God really wants me to do; the thing is to find a truth which is true for me, to find the idea for which I am willing to live and die.”

He wants to test himself. This is the only way to make progress. Living in ignorance of one’s own personal development is “intoxication and delusion,” and cannot last.

I’m reminded of why I took up journaling. It was to get some clarity about my life. I wanted to be clear on what I was to do, and a kind of understanding had to precede every action. My notes are peppered with reminders of this.

I am not yet 21, but I have a journal entry dated 22 February 2025 about Kierkegaard’s quote, and the idea that I am willing to live and die for.

So I dug it up, and here it is:

“Kierkegaard writes:

“What is truth but to live for an idea?

“I’ve been living with the lofty aim of trying to find an idea to live for and die for too. I remember my first notes about this. I got it from a lecture. Kierkegaard was a Christian existentialist. He thought that Christianity had lost the idea of really living and thinking as a Christian.

“Today I envision the idea to be working towards a community of people, where we are free to think and live. I have a lot of sympathy for Kierkegaard and what he was doing, and I see this as the logical next step. The next *natural* step, even. Because Kierkegaard’s existentialism was an act of isolation. It was a product of a mind that was unable to accept the world around him, and had to be alone to sort out what he could and could not accept.

“But I am trying to create a community of people. An idea to live for and die for.

“Kierkegaard writes:

“What I really need is to be clear about what I am to do, not what I am to know, except in the sense that a kind of understanding must precede every action. It is a question of understanding myself, of seeing what God really wants me to do; the thing is to find a truth which is true for me, to find the idea for which I am willing to live and die.”

“And so it is, but it is also a question of understanding myself in relation to others. I am trying to build a life that is a microcosm of the community that I envision. That is what I am trying to do.”
