So it used to be that I would just be online at all hours of the day. I would go to the lab at NYU, I would just get the place for myself, and I would just be coding and coding and coding. Yann LeCun was my supervisor at NYU. I was super lucky to be at the right place at the right time, at NYU right as Yann was moving there from Facebook. It was my first time meeting him, in the year 2018. I knew he was a pretty famous guy, and I didn't know how to approach him. So I just sent him an email, and then he accepted me into his lab.

I was like, "Whoa, this is man. I can't believe this is this is magical." There were like 10 other people working on AI at the lab. I got to work with him. He was super contrarian in that time. He was like, "Oh, RL this will not work. Why are you working on like model free? You should work on model based RL stuff."

Like, I was working on a project which was about using future prediction error as a reward signal. So the model would try to predict it's own states and then the loss of that prediction would be used as a loss for the policy gradient. And Yann and I were having a long discussion and I had a poster ready. It was like saying, "Hey, Yann, this is what I'm working on." And he was like, "Why are you making this so complicated?" I was like, "What do you mean?" He was like, "It's just a dopamine signal. Why are you trying to change the human learning algorithms?" I was like, "I don't know." He was like, "It's just a signal. Just make it one or zero and go from there." This was one of my projects.

I think he was right in that sense. I'm not very bullish on model-free RL now. But, I don't know if model-based RL will work. I'm not at that level of understanding of things. But, in general, I think, yeah, model-free RL is highly sample inefficient and it can't really generalize. So, yeah. It's quite interesting to be in it. And when someone like Yann LeCun, who's brilliant, gives a very strong opinion, it's quite interesting to shape your ideas around that strong opinion as a student.
