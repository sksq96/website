* Whether to roast vegetables

You get the picture.

This week, I've been reading *The Judge* by Kierkegaard. It's the second book in the Kierkegaard's Writings series, published in 1838. In it, he makes the following point:

> **My either/or does not in the first instance denote the choice between good and evil; it denotes the choice whereby one chooses good and evil/or excludes them.** Here the question is under what determinants one would contemplate the whole of existence and would himself live.

In other words, our choices structure our world. We choose, and then we live with that choice. The choice informs how we see the world, so our choices are not merely consequential, they are creative.

For instance, suppose I decide to buy a drill to hang a painting in my guest room. I can go to the hardware store and peruse all of the drills that are available. I can read reviews and ask friends, but no matter how much information I have, I still have to choose.

If I choose the PurpleDrill 3000, I'll never know how my life would have been different if I'd picked the BlueDrill 2000. I will live with the PurpleDrill 3000 and its limitations. In all likelihood, I'll never even use the PurpleDrill 3000 again, but I'll still have chosen it.

This week, I also received my Kierkegaard's Writings box set, containing all 28 books (not counting his journals and papers).

I began writing this post because I felt like I'd gotten some inkling of Kierkegaard's genius, but I'm not sure.

I haven't made it to the end of *The Judge*.

Let's see what happens.
