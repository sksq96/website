#Illusions and predictive processing

The conversation concerns, in part, predictive processing. I don’t think I understand predictive processing particularly well, but one of its **predictions** (heh) is that we ought to be able to generate lots of **illusions**. To give an example of an illusion, consider the “size-distance” illusion.

![The Müller-Lyer illusion.](/uploads/2023/illusion.png)

A classic example is shown on the left. The two horizontal lines are the same length, but the angled lines give the impression that the line on the top is longer.

I was surprised to learn that, even though I’d heard of this illusion before, I didn’t really understand it. It’s not that I didn’t understand the illusion. It’s that I’d never thought about it mechanistically. I didn’t try to explain to myself, in a mechanistic way, why the illusion should exist. It’s not that I didn’t think about it, but somehow my thoughts didn’t include trying to explain it, and so my thoughts didn’t travel down that path.

It’s interesting to think about how that could happen. How many other things do I “know” that I haven’t tried to explain to myself? The “size-distance” illusion is just one illusion. What about all the others we know of. How can we generate them? What do we think about, when trying to generate them?

In the original post, I included a link to this post by Scott Alexander, which is a pretty good summary of one predictive processing theory, this one by Anil Seth.
