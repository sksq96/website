Last night, I watched a YouTube video about the effects of meditation on concentration. This was one of the videos I watched:

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p0VM3Y44F5Q

It’s a talk about cultivating concentration in two contexts: Formal meditation (i.e., when you’re sitting and meditating) and daily life (i.e., when you’re not). Shinzen Young claims that we can have concentration in both contexts and that they are related to each other.

But he also points out that many people have concentration in one or the other. For example, some people can have a deep sense of concentration while sitting and meditating. But when they get up and start walking around and interacting with other people, their concentration can fall apart.

Other people can have deep concentration in daily life. Think of artists or athletes, for example, who can enter a flow state. But when they sit down to meditate, they start fidgeting and can’t concentrate.

Shinzen Young says we can have both if we do it right.

I’m curious to see how this affects me. And in general, I want to see what results I get from meditation. If the meditation is working, then it should have some effects. But it’s hard to notice the effects. I think it’s like people who’ve been watching too much porn. It’s hard for them to notice the effects because they’re gradual. I think the same is true for meditation.

So that’s something I’m going to notice.

I think it’s helpful to have specific things to look out for. If I ask, “What did the meditation do for me?” that’s a tough question. I don’t know what to look for. But if I ask, “Has my concentration improved?” that’s easier to answer.

So that’s what I’m going to do.
