When, if ever, would you shoot JPEG over RAW?

If I were to take a photograph that I wanted to be both, I’d want to share it in a group chat as a picture to show my evening, and share it on social media, and would want to print it and keep it in a frame. So for the former, I’d want it to be shared quickly, hence I would prefer a format that is accessible to a wide range of devices and shareable. For the latter, I would want to have a format that retains as much information as possible for me to tweak it as per my liking.

I’d prefer to keep the format in which the photograph was taken, the format that captures the most information. So I would want to keep the RAW file, and I would want to share it in whatever format my friends and group chats can handle.

The image processing engine of the camera, I don’t think mine is bad. I don’t see need for my camera to do any processing which I can do in a more powerful (and constantly improving) machine. I don’t see any compelling reason to use JPEGs in this scenario.

But if I were to think of a (non-existent) situation in which I would have a camera that can only shoot JPEGs, I would think of all the settings that I would set on my camera, and I would take the picture with those settings, and I would accept the outcome of the image processing engine.

That’s the beauty of JPEGs. The camera, instead of showing you something close to the raw data captured by the sensor, it applies the settings you tell it to the image, and shows you the processed image. As a user, you don’t have to worry about the internals of the camera. You just need to know, if the camera is in a particular setting, you will get a particular kind of output. If your camera does auto-white-balancing poorly, you can set the camera to a known white balance, and have a predictable output. But if I as a user am looking to have to have full control over the final output, it would only frustrate me to see the camera do its own processing, and then crop the output to fit into a predetermined range of values.

But, there are situations where you would want to trust the camera and its post processing engine.

Let’s say I was shooting a wildlife. I’m anticipating moments that I won’t be able to capture multiple frames of, and I won’t be able to chimp on the screen between each click.

Let’s say I have a very good handle on my camera. I know how to set it up, and I know what to expect from it.

Let’s say I’m out and about and I come across an incredible scene. It does not last very long and I want to capture it perfectly with one shot.

Let’s say I’m taking some casual pictures. I don’t want to spend time editing them. I trust my camera’s settings, I trust my camera’s post processing, and I want to take the picture and share it with people, and I don’t want to spend any more time on it.

But I would also want to have the RAW file with me for all the above situations. Because even though my camera’s image processing engine is good, my computer’s is better, and constantly improving.

Keep both, and if you have to pick one, keep the RAW, and share the JPEG.

Then my friend asked me which picture would I really want to shoot as a JPEG

This is one picture that I would want to shoot as a JPEG. I wanted to have the picture with me for the evening. I took it using my film camera, and the film is probably still sitting in the lab that is yet to develop it. I had this random camera, I don’t know how to use it very well. I also have a film camera that I know how to use very well, and I know what to expect from the film, and the camera. But I want this picture with me right now.

And I don’t care about this picture all that much. In a few days, I won’t care about it at all. I won’t print it. I won’t edit it. I might share it on social media, but honestly, I don’t care.

This camera, if it’s set to a particular setting, it gives me a particular output. I don’t believe it’s the best output. I don’t believe it’s something I won’t be able to replicate. But for now, I don’t care. I just want the picture with me. And I want it now.

This is a picture I would shoot as a JPEG.
