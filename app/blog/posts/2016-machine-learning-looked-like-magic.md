The first time I saw machine learning do something, I thought: holy fuck, this is magic.

It was in college. I was a student at IIT Mandi then, and I didn't know much. I had heard about machine learning and deep learning, but I hadn't really tried to do anything with them. Mostly I was trying to get into computer science.

Then I saw a demo, or at least read about one: DeepDream. The idea was that you take a trained neural network, look at the patterns that make it fire, and then use those patterns to create art. I had no idea how any of that worked. I just knew it felt like magic.

I could write code, and I had taken some computer science courses, but this was different. It felt like magic. Like actual magic, like the kind of thing you would see in a book written by the Egyptians, the ones with the Eye of Horus and things like that.

I had told my friends before: if I do go into computer science, I want to work on magic. That is what I want to do. And I think, in some sense, it was magic.

I was in the fifth semester by then and had started getting more serious about computer science. I had some experience with competitive programming, with Python, with coding more broadly. But I had no idea how machine learning actually worked.

Then I read about DeepDream, and I was like: holy fuck, I want to learn how the magic works.
