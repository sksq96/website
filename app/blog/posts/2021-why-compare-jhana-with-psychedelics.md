Many of my searches in December 2020 were about jhana states, and many others were about psychedelics. I thought it might be fun to compare an account of the jhana states with the written psychedelic accounts included in [here in Tim Ferriss’ work](https://tim.blog/category/psychedelics/).

My first thought was to compare individual aspects of each, such as:

- Control over attention
- Control over the sense of the passage of time
- The sense of self
- The ability to respond to the outside world
- The ability to maintain the experience
- Intensity
- Pleasure
- How common the experience is
- How stable the experience is
- Terminology

I thought that might be a bit of a tedious read, though, and the terminology isn’t really comparable anyway. So I thought I’d go over the account that stuck with me the most, which was [this one](https://tim.blog/2018/08/18/dmt-trip-report/#comment-125), and compare it to the jhana account that I am most familiar with, which is [this article by Daniel Ingram](http://www.hipforums.com/user-content.php?s=699b7a8f4f45e5abe8361cb2e6a82931f&content=2533).

I’ll quote the psychedelic account first, then explain what was similar or different about the jhana state from my understanding.

> I was so amazed, and the feeling of lifting off was extremely pleasant. The background seemed to be a humming, vibrating yellow/orange, and I was floating in this vibrating space. I had the thought that I should probably tell someone about this, but I didn’t know who. I thought maybe I could write a message on the bathroom mirror in reverse, so someone could read it later.
>
> Then the space became full of strange creatures, and I found myself telepathically in communication with them. It took a while to translate my own thoughts into their language, but eventually I communicated to them that I was a human being, and they were surprised at this. They treated me like a new, strange object, and seemed interested in my point of view. They showed me some of their toys and explained their significance. It was actually very hard to communicate, because I was trying to understand what their words meant, and this was changing the meaning of the words. It was like a logical paradox.

Two things that are of note is that the experience was not very controlled and the person’s thoughts were very prominent. In contrast, when you get into the third jhana state, you’re able to effortlessly sustain it. Your thoughts are not prominent. You feel bliss (some people call it joy) and the bliss is something separate from your thoughts.

In the fourth jhana state, it is even more detached from your thoughts as you’re detached from bliss. You’re just left with a calm, steady awareness.

> Then I concentrated really hard, and took a little time jump – maybe 20 years? And I saw a woman in a white dress. I asked the creatures if this was my wife, and they said that it was. I felt uncomfortable, and I tried to return to the present.

It seems that the tripper felt as if she had control over attention, which is like the third and fourth jhana states, but she didn’t return to the present, which is unlike the third and fourth jhana states.

> Then I returned to a normal human body, but I had the form of a dark-skinned woman (which was a surprise to me), and I was in some sort of courtyard, in a foreign place. I was very lucid, noticing details, and I had several strange conversations. I was in some sort of uniform, and I heard myself talking about the weather and some other trivial thing. I wanted to take off the uniform and return to my real body, but I didn’t know how.
>
> The woman who I was had three scars on her face, and I was in a courtyard in a foreign place. I had the feeling that I was not in my own body, and I asked someone if I was in a woman’s body. They said, “Yes, and do you know how she got those scars?” I said that I didn’t know. They said, “When she was young, she was running through a field, and she fell on some broken glass.” I was shocked, and I tried to return to my own body.

This is very different from the experience of the third and fourth jhana states. You’re completely aware of your body and the sensations in it. I don’t know anyone who has had the experience of being in another person’s body.

> Then I was in a different body, a man with a turban. Again, I was very lucid, and I was in some sort of strange place that was not familiar to me. I could not remember my real name, but somehow I remembered that I was an American woman. I asked some people if this was true, and they said that it was. But they said that my real name was Jesse. I didn’t know what to say, and I tried to return to my own body again.
>
> Then I was in my own body, but a different size than normal. I was aware that I was in my own house, but I was very small, and the room was very large. I thought that I was having a hallucination, and I tried to return to being normal. But my attempts to return to normal caused me to spiral out of control, and I saw my body as a series of connected wireframe points. I eventually got back to normal, but I was still very confused.

I’ve had the experience of being a different size, but that was only in the first jhana state, which is the most chaotic.

What this account makes me think is that the jhana states are much more controlled and stable than a psychedelic trip. If you can get into the third jhana state, you can effortlessly maintain it. It’s not chaotic like the psychedelic trip.

I’m still not sure what I wanted to compare, but I hope this was an interesting comparison to read.
