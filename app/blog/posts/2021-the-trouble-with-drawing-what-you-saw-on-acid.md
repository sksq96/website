The trouble with drawing what you saw on acid is that it’s hard to get the motion right.

Those of you who have had a chance to experience the psychedelic wonders of LSD will know precisely what I’m talking about. The rest of you will have to take my word for it.

I want to share with you this one particular instance from about an hour into my trip. I was in my room and my eyes were closed. I was watching an intricate pattern of lines unfolding on the opposite wall. The pattern was composed of nothing but parallel line segments, like the lines in a zebra crossing.

The segments would form pairs, and then those pairs would form pairs, and then those pairs would form pairs, and so on. It was an infinitely repeating pattern. The visual was 3-dimensional, and you could even see around the wall from behind it. The pattern slowly drifted around, by itself, changing and morphing. It was hypnotizing and beautiful.

When I came down to earth, hours later, I realized that this wasn’t just a hallucination, but a legit psychedelic experience.

So, I’d try to draw these things, or bits and pieces from other trips, but I never quite got the motion right. I realized that I didn’t even know how to draw simple patterns, much less draw them moving and coming alive.

To make things more interesting, let’s take a very simple pattern, like the one below, and draw it in two different ways.

![](https://i.imgur.com/Jhx7zZ3.png)

The pattern is composed of a single unit, a 3-pointed star, which is repeated in the horizontal and vertical directions. First, we’ll draw this using a regular pen on paper.

![](https://i.imgur.com/H4OOq5V.png)

I’ve always had bad handwriting, so my lines are wobbly. It’s not that bad, I’m not ashamed of it. It’s a nice drawing. You can see the jagged points. And now, we’ll draw the same pattern on my iPad, using Procreate.

![](https://i.imgur.com/eo1L7fz.png)

This is a much more regular pattern. It’s precise, clean and has a uniform stroke thickness. It’s a much more professional drawing.

So, we see two drawings of the same pattern. One is drawn by hand, the other on an iPad. Which one is better?

Clearly, the iPad drawing is better. It’s cleaner, the individual units are more well-defined and evenly spaced, and it’s uniform in every way. The handwritten one is all over the place. Well, wait a minute. The hand-drawn one is more stylistically pleasing, and it’s easier to make out the individual units.

But, the iPad one is neater and more sophisticated. Ok, yeah, the iPad one is better. Except, the handwritten one looks like it’s floating. I can almost see it moving.

It’s undeniable that the iPad one is better. But, the hand-drawn one is better too, right?

And that’s the point. There are different ways to think about the concept of “better” here.

You can think of this as a problem of conveying information. The handwritten one is a little bit sloppy, so the information isn’t conveyed as well. It doesn’t have clear edges, and the points are jagged. But, the pattern is still very much conveyed. Moreover, the wobbly lines convey a certain organic feel which the iPad one lacks. That organic feel is quite informative. It tells us something about the person who drew it, and the style with which it was created. The jagged lines give it an extra visual texture that the iPad one lacks.

It’s tempting to think that the hand-drawn one is more wasteful, with more entropy, but that isn’t true. If you think of the entropy of the drawing process, you can easily imagine that the drawing process is more random, but that is false entropy. The iPad drawing process is much more random. The stylus is a tiny stick which you control with your hand. It’s very difficult to hold it steady. The iPad is a 10-inch slab of glass, and the software helps you maintain straight lines and other shapes. The drawing process is much more deterministic, and there’s less entropy involved.

The information conveyed by both drawings is quite similar. The hand-drawn one is more stylistic, and the iPad one is more precise. Both drawings have the same information, and both are visually pleasing in their own way.

As an alternative, you can think of it in terms of the aesthetic quality. The iPad one is prettier, and that’s that. But I’d say that the hand-drawn one is also quite pleasing, in its own way.

The kind of drawing that you do when you’re on acid is more like the hand-drawn one. And the reason it’s much harder to recreate, is that every single line is moving, on its own, in its own direction. The pattern is infinitely repeating, and it’s 3-dimensional. It’s like a living thing, constantly changing and shifting. And you’re trying to capture that on paper. It’s a very difficult task.

Now, I’m going to try to draw my trip.
