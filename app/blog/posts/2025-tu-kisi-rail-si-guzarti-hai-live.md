I've been listening to this song for a while now, as you can probably see from the list of dates. There are plenty of versions of the song, which is from a ghazal by Faiz Ahmed Faiz. But I've liked this one the best.

This is the second time I've seen the magic of Indian Ocean, which came to my attention after I saw the top 100 movies on Letterboxd. _Swades_ was on the list, and I was surprised to see that a lot of the sound design was done by a band I was only vaguely familiar with.

Indian Ocean is a band that has been around since the early 1990s and has played an integral role in India's folk rock movement.

I don't know what it is about this song, but it's really stuck with me. I think it's Rahul Ramakrishnan's voice, but the combination of instruments has created a sound that I really love. There's a point in the song, at the refrain, when the instruments come in full force, and it's just magical.

I would probably pick the moment from 3:00 to 3:20, which is when the melody of the song is laid out.

There are a lot of ways to interpret this ghazal, but my take is this:

The lover is saying that getting to know the beloved is too difficult. The beloved is too far away, too inaccessible, too mysterious. It would be easier if the beloved were more concrete, more legible. It would be easier if she could leave a trail that the lover could follow. But instead, she is like a train that passes by. The lover is left with just the marks of the train, but not the train itself.
