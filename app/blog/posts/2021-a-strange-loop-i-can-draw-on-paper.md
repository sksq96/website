I am currently reading Gödel, Escher, Bach , aka GEB, written by Douglas Hofstadter in 1979.  I'm enjoying it immensely, except that I have to look up a ton of references. Bach is my favorite composer, but Escher and Gödel are less familiar to me. How much do you think you know about the history of the piano? I thought I knew a lot, but reading this book has given me a deeper understanding of the piano, Bach, and his contemporaries.

There are details and meta-details in this book that I need to look up and spend some time with to properly understand. This is not a bad thing, because I think my understanding of the world is better informed for it, but it has meant progress through the book is slow. I am about 50% through, so I've spent 2-3 months so far.

I recently watched a lecture by Hofstadter, titled "What gets me out of bed in the morning". The talk is about creativity, and in it he talks about his book, where I heard the term "strange loop". A strange loop is a feature, and he uses it to describe characters in the many worlds of Bach.

I found a paper on strange loops. It's written by Hofstadter, and can be found here.

I do not believe I understand strange loops yet. What I've learned so far is that strange loops are not common, they are not cycles. A loop is a constraint that can exist in time or space, and to my current understanding, while non-strange loops can exist in many contexts, strange loops have only been demonstrated in "art", for example music, paintings, and literature.

I am curious to know if anyone can give me an example of a strange loop that I can draw on a piece of paper or on a whiteboard, that others can follow and understand.
